### DEC-001 — Own an Ashley-style component model rather than depending on Ashley or Artemis
The simulation uses an in-house, component-based data-oriented model with a fixed explicit system schedule, rather than adopting Ashley (libGDX's ECS) or Artemis-odb.

### DEC-002 — Chose the ray-cast vehicle model over rigid-body wheels
Vehicles use `btRaycastVehicle` — one rigid body plus N ray-cast wheels — rather than rigid-body wheels joined by `btHinge2Constraint`.

### DEC-003 — glTF 2.0 (.glb) is the runtime mesh format, chosen for morph target support
Processed meshes ship as glTF 2.0 binary (`.glb`), imported with gdx-gltf, because it is the only well-supported format that carries morph targets as a first-class feature.

### DEC-004 — A vehicle is one rigid body with a compound shape, not one body per part
Each vehicle is a single `btRigidBody` whose collision shape is a `btCompoundShape` of per-part convex hulls. Parts become independent bodies only at the moment they detach.

### DEC-005 — Shard trajectories are cosmetic and are never replicated
The authority replicates the *fact* of a fracture (part, tick, parent velocity) but never individual shard transforms or velocities. Each client spawns its own shard set locally.

### DEC-006 — Blueprint invariants are enforced by Gradle check tasks, not by convention
Every mechanically checkable rule in D02 and D00 is implemented as a Gradle task in `build-logic/` and wired into `check`, rather than left as a documented convention. The module catalogue of D02-S4.5 is duplicated as data in `ModuleRules.kt` so the checks have something to compare against.

### DEC-007 — ECS interface is EntitySystem; LaunchConfig belongs to shared-models
Two placements the blueprints left open: the ECS system interface is `EntitySystem`, not `System`, and `LaunchConfig` plus its resolver live in `shared-models` (`dev.syndicate.model.config`) rather than in `game-core`.

### DEC-008 — The harness parses GLB itself rather than importing it through gdx-gltf
`test-environment` reads vertex positions and indices out of `.glb` with its own reader (`GlbReader`, ~200 lines) instead of loading the file through gdx-gltf. The decisive reason is that gdx-gltf builds libGDX `Mesh` objects, which are GPU buffers requiring a GL context — and D14-S5.13 requires the headless runner to create none.

### DEC-009 — MAX_SHARDS_PER_PART raised from 64 to 256
The hard cap on shards per part goes from 64 to 256. The old value was chosen before there was a fracture implementation to measure; the exact half-space path makes a 100-shard fracture cost 2 seconds and hold every correctness property, so the cap was bounding nothing real.

### DEC-010 — CI runs the D12-S5.4 stages as steps in one job, not one job per stage
The workflow ran three jobs (stages 0, 1-2, 7) and triggered on both `push: ["**"]` and `pull_request`, so every commit on a branch with an open PR ran the whole pipeline twice: 6 jobs, 573 s of runner time, for one commit's worth of information. It is now a single job whose steps are the stages, triggered on `pull_request` plus `push` to `main` only.

### DEC-011 — A non-convex source is decomposed by solid BSP, exactly, not by V-HACD
A non-convex source is split into disjoint convex pieces by a solid binary space partition over its own face planes, so `cell ∩ source` becomes a union of exact polytope intersections. An approximate decomposition (V-HACD) and a per-cell mesh boolean were both rejected: each gives up the exactness that is the point of the convex path.

### DEC-012 — PhysicsWorld is injected into PhysicsSystem, and cross-tick impulses queue on it
`PhysicsWorld` is a constructor dependency of `PhysicsSystem` rather than a field on `World`, and the "pending impulses" of D06-S5.4 step 1 are a queue owned by `PhysicsWorld`, drained and applied by `PhysicsSystem` in ascending entity id order.

### DEC-013 — test-environment does not depend on gdx-gltf; D02-S4.5 amended to match
`gdx-gltf` is removed from `test-environment`'s dependencies and moved to its "MUST NOT depend on" column in D02-S4.5. No source in the repository ever imported it — DEC-008 made it unused — and it was the one artifact keeping the module from building without JitPack.

### DEC-014 — An attached part's mass lives in a bodyless RigidBodyComponent
A part that is attached to a vehicle carries a `RigidBodyComponent` whose `body` is null and whose `massKg` and `centerOfMassLocal` are its authoritative mass properties. `MassPropertySystem` sums those fields; nothing else in the component catalogue holds a part's mass at runtime.

### DEC-015 — Per-vehicle compounds and their child-index maps are owned by ShapeCache
A vehicle's `btCompoundShape` and its slot-path-to-child-index map live in a `VehicleCompound` registered in `ShapeCache` under the key `(assemblyId, COMPOUND, entityIndex)`, and are released by `EntityDestroySystem` when the vehicle dies. They are the one instance-scoped, mutable entry in a cache that is otherwise immutable and shared.

### DEC-016 — detachPart is a shared operation, and slot 15 triggers on its own output
`detachPart` lives in `dev.syndicate.core.vehicle.PartDetachment` as a static operation over components, called by both `FractureSystem` (13) and the future `DetachSystem` (14). It does **not** call `MassPropertySystem.recompute`: slot 15 detects the change itself, by comparing the mass and COM it computes against the values stored on `VehicleChassisComponent`.

### DEC-017 — wreckVehicle does not fracture the chassis; slot 13 does, one slot earlier
D07-S5.7's `wreckVehicle` calls `fracturePart` on the chassis, which D04-R13 prohibits as a system-to-system call. `FractureSystem` (13) instead fractures a destroyed chassis part like any other — without detaching it, because the chassis cannot be detached (D05-R26) — and `DetachSystem` (14) treats a chassis part that is *gone or destroyed* as the wreck trigger, one slot later in the same tick.

### DEC-018 — a detached part becomes debris from its own state, not from its trigger
`PartDetachment.detach` records each leaving part's world transform and the velocity it left with (`v + ω × r`) on the part's own `Transform` and `Velocity`, and `DetachSystem` (14) turns every part that is `DETACHED`, vehicle-less and bodyless into a debris body in one pass keyed on that state. Neither half asks which trigger removed the part, or whether slot 14 removed it at all.

### DEC-019 — the chassis is a field on the assembly record, not a row in its parts list
`AssemblyDef` carries `chassisPartTypeId` as its own field and lists only the attached parts in `parts`, following D08-S4.4's file format rather than D05-S5.1's validation pseudocode, which reads the root out of `parts` by looking for a null `parentSlotPath`.

### DEC-020 — MASS_DELTA_FRAC aliases MASS_TOLERANCE_FRAC, and COM_OFFSET_M is 0.01 m
D08 names two tolerance constants — `MASS_DELTA_FRAC` and `COM_OFFSET_M` — that D00-S6.4 never defines. `AssemblyValidator.MASS_DELTA_FRAC` is an alias of `MASS_TOLERANCE_FRAC` (0.02), and `COM_OFFSET_M` is fixed at 0.01 m, one collision margin.

### DEC-021 — the spawn path establishes mass properties itself; slot 15 confirms them
`VehicleFactory` computes total mass and centre of mass, recentres the compound on the COM, and writes both to `VehicleChassisComponent` and `RigidBodyComponent` as part of building the chassis body — rather than calling `MassPropertySystem.recompute` as D05-S5.2 step 4 does. Slot 15 then compares equal on the spawn tick and does nothing.

### DEC-022 — wheel radius comes from the collision mesh; suspension length is a constant
`btRaycastVehicle.addWheel` needs a wheel radius and a suspension rest length, and no blueprint authors either. The radius is derived from the wheel's collision mesh — half the larger of its Y and Z extents — and the rest length is a constant, `WHEEL_SUSPENSION_REST_LENGTH_M = 0.3`.

### DEC-023 — a progress entry tracks one subsystem, and supersession never crosses subsystems
`progress/` holds one active entry per subsystem — simulation, destruction toolchain, content and runtime shells — and a new entry supersedes only the entry for the same subsystem. PROG-001 to PROG-006 formed a single global chain instead, which had already hidden a working subsystem behind a superseded entry.

### DEC-024 — the degradation table is exhaustive, and a multiplier fades toward identity
A `(category, stat)` pair the D05-S5.4 DEGRADATION_TABLE does not name does not degrade, and a stat's `mul` term fades toward 1 rather than toward 0: `mul' = 1 + (mul - 1) · m`. Both are questions D05-S5.4 leaves open once its scalar pseudocode meets `StatBlock`'s add/mul pairs.

### DEC-025 — slot 6 recomputes every tick, and the power budget is over live parts
`VehicleStatsSystem` aggregates every vehicle on every tick rather than gating on `VehicleStats.dirty` or on a remembered `structuralVersion`, and its `powerBudget` sums the live parts rather than the whole assembly.

### DEC-026 — steering state lives on the chassis, and every vehicle carries a PlayerInput
`VehicleChassisComponent` gains `currentSteerRad` and `VehicleStatsComponent` gains `maxSteerRad`; D04-S4.3.2 was amended in the same commit for both. `VehicleFactory` also adds a `PlayerInputComponent` to every vehicle, which D04-R4's `VEHICLE` archetype requires and D05-S5.2's pseudocode omits.

### DEC-027 — steer lock, steer rate, drag and rolling resistance are named constants
Four numbers D05-S5.6 needs and no document authors: `DEFAULT_MAX_STEER_RAD = 0.5236` (30°), `DEFAULT_STEER_RATE_RAD_PER_SEC = 1.05`, `CHASSIS_DRAG_COEFFICIENT = 12.0` N per (m/s)², and `CHASSIS_ROLLING_RESISTANCE = 0.015`. All four live on `VehicleStatsSystem` with the reasoning below.

### DEC-028 — a destroyed wheel is commanded to zero, not skipped
D06-S5.5's control loop reads `if w.destroyed: continue`. `VehicleControlSystem` instead writes zero engine force, zero steering and zero grip to a destroyed wheel, because Bullet's per-wheel setters are persistent rather than per-step.

### DEC-029 — the wheel tuning defaults are D06-S4.5's, not Bullet's forklift demo's
`VehicleFactory`'s per-wheel constants now carry D06-S4.5's reference chassis values. DEC-022 chose Bullet demo figures on the reading that no blueprint authored them; D06-S4.5's R12 table authors all of them.

### DEC-030 — the catalogue lives in game-core; client systems arrive via a provider
`SystemSlot` holds all 27 slots of D04-S4.4 with their phase, module and mode filter; `SystemSetFactory.forMode` filters it and asks a `SystemProvider` to construct each surviving slot. `CoreSystemProvider` builds the `game-core` systems; the six `game-client` slots are contributed by a provider the client passes in.

### DEC-031 — a part authors physics it cannot express as a stat in a handling block
`HandlingBlock` carries the nine physical parameters D06-S4.5's reference chassis table names and D05-S4.5's stat list cannot hold: drag, rolling resistance and downforce on a chassis; damping, roll influence and suspension geometry on a wheel. It is authored as a `handling` object on the part, and D08-R5 was amended in the same commit.

### DEC-032 — tractive force is capped by engine power, so a vehicle has a top speed
A fifteenth stat, `enginePowerW`, joins D05-S4.5's table. Available tractive force is `min(engineForceN, enginePowerW / v)`: traction-limited at rest, power-limited once moving. Without it, a chassis calibrated to a real car's 0-100 time reports a top speed several times what that car has.

### DEC-033 — a vehicle's physics come from a real car; its name does not
`VehicleProfile` carries a `displayName` for the in-game vehicle and a separate `referenceVehicle` naming the real car its figures came from. The Apex GT is derived from a Maserati MC20 and the Stampede GT3 from a Ford Mustang GT3; neither manufacturer's name appears in an asset id, a display name or the shipped content.

### DEC-034 — brake force is split across wheels by the load each is carrying
D06-S5.5 divides brake force equally across the wheels. `VehicleControlSystem` divides it in proportion to each wheel's live suspension load instead, which is what an electronic brake-force distribution does and what makes a shipped vehicle stop in roughly its published distance.

### DEC-035 — the glTF reader lives in game-core and composes node transforms
`GltfReader` is a `game-core` class in `dev.syndicate.core.asset`, it composes every node's transform down from the scene root and applies it to the geometry it returns, and `test-environment`'s `GlbReader` becomes a thin adapter over it rather than a second parser.

### DEC-036 — a model's unit and axis correction lives in import.json, verified
The correction that takes a piece of downloaded art into the game's units and axes is recorded once, in an `import.json` beside the model — scale, then yaw about Y, then translation — and the harness's `--model` mode applies it *before* it measures, so the numbers in that file are confirmed by the checks rather than asserted by a comment.

### DEC-037 — the Stampede comes from a Mustang GTD, so the pair's contrast is mass
`STAMPEDE`'s reference car changes from the 1289 kg Ford Mustang GT3 to the 1969 kg Ford Mustang GTD, because the GTD is the car the supplied art actually is. The shipped pair is therefore a light car against a heavy one rather than a road car against a race car, and the calibration tests assert the new contrast rather than the old.

### DEC-038 — the damage pipeline is a shared operation, and slot 12 is only its schedule slot
The armour formulas, the damage state machine and the propagation walk live in `dev.syndicate.core.damage.DamageApplication`, a static-shaped operation over components in the mould of `PartDetachment` (DEC-016). `DamageSystem` (12) owns the schedule slot, the event ordering and the burn timer, and nothing else.

### DEC-039 — a weapon's identity is a block; its numbers are stats
`WeaponBlock` carries what a weapon *is* — family, damage type, ammunition capacity, blast radius, range, muzzle offset — and D05-S4.5's stat block keeps what a weapon *does*: fire interval, damage per shot, spread, heat, projectile speed. D08-R5 was amended in the same commit, exactly as it was for `HandlingBlock` (DEC-031).

### DEC-040 — the damage ledger is a bucketed window on the match singleton
`DamageLedger` keeps a match-long total and a ten-second window per attacker-victim pair, the window as ten fixed buckets of sixty ticks rather than a log of every hit. It lives in a `DamageLedgerComponent` on the match singleton (entity 1), and D04-S4.3.5 was amended to name it.

### DEC-041 — the asset pipeline validates independently of the runtime loader
`asset-pipeline`'s `AssetIndexBuilder` reads `assets/` with its own Jackson walk rather than calling `game-core`'s `AssetLoader`. The two share the D08-S5.4 rule codes and nothing else.

### DEC-042 — a vehicle is dissected by geometry, never by the names in the file
`syndicate_dissect` finds a vehicle's wheels by connected-component analysis and spatial classification — outboard, low, and round in profile — and ignores every name in the source file. It is a second Blender package beside `syndicate_fracture` rather than a mode of it.

### DEC-043 — a part's mass acts at its collision mesh's centre, not at its slot
`PartType.centerOfMassLocal` is the AABB centre of the part's collision mesh, and both `AssemblyLayout` and `MassPropertySystem` (15) weight a part's mass there rather than at its attachment point. D06-S5.7's pseudocode already says `part.type.comLocal`; the implementation was using the slot transform's translation, which is a different point on every part that is not centred on its own origin.

### DEC-044 — what turns with a wheel is decided by rotational symmetry
A piece captured into a wheel corner rotates with the wheel only if its geometry occupies at least `ROTATION_SYMMETRY_MIN_DEG` (300°) of the circle about the axle, measured over vertices in 15° sectors. Everything else — the brake caliper — is returned to the chassis.

### DEC-045 — the material says what a part is made of; the part says how it fails
`assets/materials/materials.json` is the single shared authority for density, per-damage-type resistance, fracture brittleness and — new — `audioMaterial`. `destructionClass` is **not** on it: it is a property of what a part *is*, so it lives on `part.json` and defaults from `PartCategory`.

### DEC-046 — the sound bank is synthesised, and the reason is the licence
Every sound in `assets/audio/` is generated by `asset-pipeline`'s `SoundBankBuilder` rather than sampled or downloaded. D15-R38 ranks procedural synthesis second behind permissively licensed libraries; the bank takes it first, for every family rather than only the loops.

### DEC-047 — an engine is keyed on its configuration, not on its vehicle's weight class
Engine loops are generated one per `EngineConfiguration` — I4, I6, V6, V8, V10, V12 — and a vehicle's own `EngineVoice` (configuration, idle rpm, redline rpm, peak power) decides the pitch, gain and low-end weight it is played at. The first cut keyed them on `light`/`medium`/`heavy` and was wrong.

### DEC-048 — gamepad and keyboard are peers, and the active one is observed
`InputCollectionSystem` (slot 1) polls both a `GamepadSource` and a `KeyboardMouseSource` every frame and keeps the output of whichever device the player last touched. Neither is primary. Tuning is content, in `assets/input/bindings.json`.

### DEC-049 — a PRESENT system is handed frame time, and reads alpha off the world
`World.present(alpha, frameDtSeconds)` passes the real frame delta as a PRESENT system's `dtSeconds` and exposes the render interpolation factor separately through `World.renderAlpha()`. The single-argument `present(alpha)`, which passed alpha as the dt, is gone.

### DEC-050 — slot 22 interpolates the ticks this process ran, not snapshots from a wire
`InterpolationSystem` (slot 22) samples every entity's world transform once per tick and rebuilds a render transform `alpha` of the way between the last two samples. It does not read `InterpolationComponent`'s snapshot ring buffer, which stays empty until slot 19 exists to fill it.

### DEC-051 — the client attaches its own cosmetic components; core factories never do
`RenderTransformComponent`, `RenderModelComponent` and `DamageVisualComponent` are attached by the client systems that own them — slots 22, 26 and 23 respectively — each through a family that excludes the component it is about to add. `VehicleFactory` and `DebrisFactory` attach none of them.

### DEC-052 — an engine is a pulse train through an exhaust, not a harmonic stack
`SoundSynth.engineLoop` builds an engine from one unipolar exhaust pulse per firing event, placed at its crank angle and routed to its own bank, then run through three fixed exhaust formants — rather than stacking harmonics of the firing frequency. How far a V's two banks diverge is derived from `EngineConfiguration.bankFiringSpreadDegrees()`, not from a global constant. See DEC-054 for that second half.

### DEC-053 — induction is a second voice, keyed on a closed set like the configuration is
A new `Induction` enum — `NATURALLY_ASPIRATED`, `TURBO`, `SUPERCHARGED` — joins `EngineConfiguration` on `EngineVoice`. The bank holds one loop per forced member plus a release for the one that has one; the runtime derives pitch and gain per vehicle. D15-S8 was amended in the same commit (R36a–c).

### DEC-054 — bank divergence scales with how unevenly a bank fires
`SoundSynth.bankDivergence` returns 0.10 for an arrangement whose banks fire identically and 1.0 for the cross-plane V8, interpolated on `bankFiringSpreadDegrees() / 180`. It scales both the second bank's formant detune and its manifold delay. A global constant does not work, and the two ways it fails are opposite.

### DEC-055 — engines get their own audio device and mixer, not libGDX Sound handles
Engine voices are rendered into `EngineMixer` and written to a `Gdx.audio.newAudioDevice(48000, false)` stereo sink on a dedicated daemon thread. Every other sound in the bank stays on libGDX `Sound` exactly as it was. Positioning — distance, pan, air absorption, propagation delay and the doppler that falls out of it — is ours, applied inside the mixer.

### DEC-056 — the engine is synthesised at runtime; 27 of the bank's 74 files are deleted
`EngineSynth` in `game-client` integrates the crank angle forward at the simulation's rpm and fires cylinders as they come round, a block at a time from the audio callback. Six engine loops, eighteen start/stop/overrun one-shots and three induction files are deleted; the bank goes 74 → 47. `EngineVoice` loses its sound ids, its pitch ratio and `REFERENCE_RPM`; `AudioEvent` loses six members.

### DEC-057 — a V's gain imbalance scales with bank unevenness and has no floor
The detune and the manifold delay between a V's banks keep their 10% floor, so no two banks are ever identical. The **gain** imbalance loses it: it now scales purely with how unevenly the banks fire, which is zero for every arrangement here except the cross-plane V8.

### DEC-058 — the client's acknowledgement rides on its input, and a NACK is a new message
`InputCommand` gains an `acknowledgedSnapshotTick` field, and a new `SnapshotNack` message joins the D10-S4.2 catalogue. D10-S4.2 was amended in the same commit.

### DEC-059 — a vehicle's network ids are one contiguous block, derived not sent
`SpawnEntity` carries a base network id and a block size. The vehicle takes the base, its chassis part the next id, and every other part the ids after that in ascending slot-path order — a walk both peers perform for themselves in `ReplicationTagging.replicatedEntities`.

### DEC-060 — a part is exactly as relevant as the vehicle it is attached to
`Relevance.isRelevantTo` answers for a part by asking the same question of its vehicle, rather than measuring the part's own distance or treating it as an unknown archetype.

### DEC-061 — vehicle control becomes a shared operation so reconciliation can replay it
The body of `VehicleControlSystem` (slot 7) moves to `dev.syndicate.core.vehicle.VehicleControl`. The system keeps the slot, the family and its public constants; `ReconciliationSystem` (20) holds its own instance.

### DEC-062 — a client sends its input from slot 19, because slot 18 is authority-only
`NetworkReceiveSystem` (19) records the tick's prediction, sends the `InputCommand` packet, and then applies whatever arrived. D04-S4.4's row for slot 19 was amended in the same commit to say so.

### DEC-063 — D15's slot roles are not SlotTypes, and a door is an armour panel
The preparation pipeline maps each D15-S4.1 label onto a real `PartCategory` and a real `SlotType`, and D15-S4.1 was amended in the same commit to carry both columns. A `panel` — which is what a door, a bonnet and a bumper all are — is exported as an `ARMOR` part in an `ARMOR_PANEL` slot.

### DEC-064 — a prepared vehicle is weighed from its footprint; the chassis takes the balance
A dropped-in model has a kerb mass nobody measured and a power budget nobody authored. The pipeline derives both: mass per part from `area × wall thickness × density` (DISC-035) with the **chassis taking whatever is left** of a target derived from the vehicle's footprint, and `powerCost` **distributed** across the parts so the assembly's budget equals its class's target exactly.

### DEC-065 — the pipeline writes import.json, and writes the composition not the residual
`syndicate-prepare` derives the scale, yaw and translation that put a model in the game's frame and records them in the model's own `import.json`. DEC-036 stands unchanged — that file is still the single recorded correction and still what the harness verifies — but an operator no longer has to author it before the pipeline will run.

### DEC-066 — a piece turns with a wheel if it covers the circle **or** repeats around it
DEC-044's angular-coverage test gains a second, independently sufficient one: a material group in a wheel corner also rotates if its occupied sectors are invariant under a turn of `360/n` for some `n ≥ 3`. Neither test replaces the other; a piece rotates if it passes either.

### DEC-067 — a part's mass is its surface times a per-class areal density
The per-class mass constant is a **mass per square metre**, not a wall thickness times the material's density, and the enclosed volume caps the result. DEC-064's mass formula is superseded; the rest of that entry stands unchanged.

### DEC-068 — doors are found by a geometric cue, not by per-model overrides
The Stampede's doors were labelled `chassis`. They are added to C1 as a **door-shaped** geometric vote rather than named in the Stampede's `parts.json`, because a pipeline whose promise is "drop in a model" cannot require a human to measure boxes for each new model.

### DEC-069 — the ground is a height field derived from a seed, not a mesh and not replicated
An arena's ground is a regular height field generated at load from `(arenaId, terrainSeed)`, given to Bullet as a `btHeightfieldTerrainShape`, and **derived independently by every peer** rather than sent over the wire. The two rejected alternatives are a loaded triangle-mesh arena and a replicated heightmap.

### DEC-070 — per-surface grip is applied at the wheel, not through Bullet's material callback
The surface under a wheel changes that wheel's `frictionSlip` and rolling resistance inside the shared vehicle control operation (DEC-061), looked up from the terrain's surface grid at the suspension ray's contact point. It does **not** go through the terrain body's Bullet friction, and it does not go through `CF_CUSTOM_MATERIAL_CALLBACK`.

### DEC-071 — a structure is an assembly, so nothing new breaks it
A destructible structure — a barrier, a sign gantry, a fuel bowser — is specified as an **assembly** in the D05 sense: a root part with parts on its slots, ordinary `part.json` entries, ordinary fracture manifests from the ordinary D09 tool. It differs from a vehicle in exactly one way: one **static body per part**, rather than one dynamic body carrying a compound of every part (DEC-004).

### DEC-072 — menu type is rasterised by FreeType, with the bitmap font as a fallback
`game-client` gains `gdx-freetype`, and `assets/fonts/` gains Oswald under the SIL Open Font License. Menu text is rasterised at the pixel size it is drawn at, cached per size. If the native or the font file is missing, every request returns libGDX's built-in bitmap font and the game still starts.

### DEC-073 — `ARMOR` names two different things, and the collision is left for the user
The word *armour* currently covers both "bolted-on protective plating a player chooses" (D01, D05) and "any body panel — a door, a bonnet, a bumper" (DEC-063, the preparation pipeline). Both readings are defensible, they are not the same concept, and the ambiguity is now load-bearing in the taxonomy. This entry records the collision and the options; **the choice is the user's and has not been made.**

### DEC-074 — an arena is a theme and a seed, and the seed defaults to the match's
`TerrainParams.Biome` is replaced by a top-level `ArenaTheme` that owns the relief layer, the surface palette, the ground albedo and every generator number. An arena declares a theme and a seed and gets a coherent landscape. A seed of zero means "derive one from the match seed", so a theme generates maps rather than being one map.

### DEC-075 — a vehicle owns its parts; the shared library is what is modular
A part now lives in one of two places, and which one says what it is for. `assets/vehicles/<vehicleTypeId>/parts/<partTypeId>/` holds a **vehicle-owned** part — a chassis, a door, a wheel — and only that vehicle's assembly may reference it (A315). `assets/parts/` becomes the **shared library**: weapons, utility modules, universal accessories, fitted to anything with the right slot. The flat `assets/parts/` that held both is gone.

### DEC-076 — the house style is a table, applied to materials before anything is measured
`assets/materials/style.json` fixes, per surface role, the colour band and reflectance every vehicle in the game is moved into. `syndicate_prepare` applies it as stage 1b — after the model loads, before the geometry is corrected — so a photoscanned supercar and a flat-shaded cartoon come out looking like one artist's work.

### DEC-077 — a built-in weapon is derived, a modular one is authored
Weapons arrive two ways and the project now expresses both. A **built-in** weapon is geometry the model came with — a tank's barrel — labelled `weapon` by the preparation pipeline, exported as a `WEAPON` part under its own vehicle, with a `weapon` block derived from its shape. A **modular** weapon or utility module is authored content in the shared library and fits any vehicle, because every prepared vehicle now offers five mounting points its own art never supplied.

### DEC-078 — a lamp is content, its light is cosmetic, and a beam is two things
A `light` part is authored with a `light` block giving its colour, cone, range, direction, origin and whether it casts. The client reads that block itself, places a spot light per lit lamp, and draws a translucent cone from the lens. `game-core` never learns any of it.

### DEC-079 — a palette and a tone band, not just per-surface rows
The house style gains two mechanisms that apply to **every** surface, on top of the per-surface rows: hues are snapped onto a six-hue palette, and finished luminance is clamped into one global band. The style is retargeted at Crossout's wasteland — Syndicate industrial plate and Ravager black-and-cyan — as `style_syndicate_wasteland`.

### DEC-080 — a weapon is an assembly, and its sub-parts hang off a synthesised mount
A modular weapon is a **slot graph**, not a part: a `mount` occupying the vehicle's hardpoint, with a `receiver`, `barrel`, `muzzle`, `breech`, `feed`, `gear`, `sight` and `furniture` hanging off it on `SUBSLOT`s, each with its own mass, health and damage state. The `mount` carries the `weapon` block and is the part that fires; every other sub-part is a target that can be shot off independently. Where a model supplies no mount — which is most gun models — one is **synthesised as real geometry**.

### DEC-081 — size class gates bulk, mass gates load, and they are separate numbers
`SizeClass` — `LIGHT`, `MEDIUM`, `HEAVY` — is carried by both a slot definition and a part type, and **a slot accepts its own class and every class below it**. It sits alongside the two gates that already existed rather than replacing either: `SlotType` decides *what kind* of thing attaches, size class decides *how bulky*, and `maxMassKg` decides *how heavy*. A violation is `A316`.

### DEC-082 — recoil and knockback are one momentum formula, and a rocket is its one exception
Firing shoves the firer and being hit shoves the target, both from the same figure — the shot's momentum, `projectileMassKg × speed` — and both applied **off the centre of mass**: recoil at the muzzle, knockback at the contact point. `WeaponFamily` gains `projectileMassKg`, `nominalSpeedMps` and `recoilFraction`, and `recoilFraction` is 0 for exactly one family.

### DEC-083 — articulation is one block with five motions, extended rather than duplicated
Weapon movement reuses the `articulation` block D15-S5.6 already defined for doors, extended with a `motion` discriminator — `HINGE`, `RECOIL`, `SPIN`, `INDEX`, `ELEVATE` — and a `driver` — `OPEN`, `FIRE`, `CONTINUOUS`, `AIM`. `HINGE` and `OPEN` are the defaults, so every door part both shipped vehicles already carry keeps working with no file rewritten. It is **cosmetic** (G6), read by `game-client` and never by `game-core`.

### DISC-001 — Configuration cache rejects onlyIf lambdas reading a script-level val
With `org.gradle.configuration-cache=true`, an `onlyIf { }` spec in a `.gradle.kts` file fails to serialise if its body reads a top-level `val` or an unqualified `logger`. The fix is to copy the value into a local inside the task's configure block; the diagnostic never says this.

### DISC-002 — Blender writes to stdout at the C level, breaking the tool's JSON-only contract
D09-R2 requires the tool's stdout to carry exactly one JSON document and nothing else. Blender does not cooperate: its glTF exporter writes a Draco availability notice to the process's stdout at the C level on every run, landing in the middle of the document an agent is about to parse. The tool now duplicates the real stdout to a private descriptor and points fd 1 at stderr.

### DISC-003 — bpy module teardown segfaults, overwriting the tool's exit code
When the tool runs on the `bpy` PyPI module (DEV-002), normal interpreter shutdown after a run that loaded and freed meshes segfaults inside Blender's own teardown. The process then exits 139 regardless of what the tool decided, destroying the exit-code contract D09-S4.3 exists to provide. The entry point calls `os._exit` after flushing instead.

### DISC-004 — btShapeHull.buildHull(margin) double-counts the collision margin
Passing a shape's collision margin to `btShapeHull.buildHull(margin)` offsets the generated hull points outward by that margin, and the `btConvexHullShape` built from the result then adds its own margin on top. A simplified hull therefore has its collision surface two margins outside its mesh while an unsimplified one has exactly one. Pass `0`.

### DISC-005 — an unanchored `build/` in .gitignore silently untracked build-logic
`.gitignore` carried a bare `build/`. Git matches an unanchored pattern against a directory of that name at **any depth**, and `build-logic`'s guardrail tasks live in package `dev.syndicate.build` — so `build-logic/src/main/kotlin/dev/syndicate/build/` was ignored and all seven task classes were never committed. The working tree built perfectly; a clean clone failed to configure with `Unresolved reference: dev`.

### DISC-006 — the point-in-mesh ray test double-counts a ray grazing a shared edge
`fracture._inside_predicate` counts a `+X` ray crossing by triangle parity, and a ray passing exactly along the edge two triangles share is counted **twice** — so the point reads as outside when it is inside. Its docstring claims a tie-break resolves this; there is none.

### DISC-007 — the sandbox cannot provision a JDK 17 toolchain; api.foojay.io is blocked
In the remote agent sandbox the only JDK is 21, and the foojay resolver that would download the pinned JDK 17 (D02-R1) cannot reach `api.foojay.io` — the egress proxy answers the CONNECT with 403. Every Gradle invocation then fails during configuration with a message about the configuration cache, not about the toolchain.

### DISC-008 — btShapeHull bakes the source shape's margin into its points
`btShapeHull` samples support points from the shape it is handed, and those include that shape's collision margin. Its `buildHull(margin)` argument is ignored. So a source shape carrying a margin yields hull points already pushed one margin outward, and the finished shape adds its own on top — a simplified hull sits two margins outside its mesh, an unsimplified one exactly one. Supersedes the remedy recorded in DISC-004.

### DISC-009 — btShapeHull has no target vertex count; it reduces to 42 and no further
`btShapeHull` cannot be asked for a vertex budget. It samples support points along a fixed set of 42 unit-sphere directions and keeps the unique ones, so its output is bounded at 42 whatever the caller wants. `MAX_HULL_VERTICES` (64) is therefore always satisfied and `MAX_SHARD_HULL_VERTICES` (32) cannot be enforced at runtime at all.

### DISC-010 — Bullet freezes a body's deactivation clock the moment it falls asleep
`btCollisionObject::getDeactivationTime()` accumulates while a body is below the sleeping thresholds and stops the instant the body reaches `ISLAND_SLEEPING`, so it plateaus at Bullet's own `gDeactivationTime` of 2 s and can never reach the 3 s `SLEEP_DESPAWN_S` asks for. `LifetimeSystem` advances it itself for sleeping bodies.

### DISC-011 — Bullet ray tests are locked to DefaultFilter, so a wheel finds no ground
`btDefaultVehicleRaycaster` builds its `ClosestRayResultCallback` internally, on filter group `btBroadphaseProxy::DefaultFilter` (`1<<0`), and offers no way to change it. Bullet's filter test is bidirectional, so a body whose mask omits that bit is invisible to the suspension ray — and D06-S4.4's `STATIC` mask omits it.

### DISC-012 — Bullet reads setBrake as an impulse and applyEngineForce as a force
`btRaycastVehicle::updateFriction` multiplies `m_engineForce` by the timestep and uses `m_brake` directly as a maximum impulse. Passing a force to `setBrake` therefore brakes `1/TICK_DT` — sixty times — too hard.

### DISC-013 — a test resolving LaunchConfig from System.getenv asserts the machine
CI exports `SYNDICATE_STRICT_ASSETS=1` for the whole job. `ServerMain.run` resolved its configuration from `System.getenv()`, so two `ServerMainTest` cases that assert the non-strict asset path passed locally and failed on CI, expecting `OK` and getting `ASSETS_INVALID`.

### DISC-014 — a published braking ordering does not survive a change of mass
The Stampede's published 100-0 is 30 m against the Eclipse's 33 m, and in simulation it stops **longer** — 34.9 m against 33.7 m. A ray-cast wheel's brake is clipped to its own friction circle, so 31% more mass beats 25% more grip and the ordering inverts.

### DISC-015 — a Bullet contact point carries the compound child index in getIndex0/getIndex1
`btManifoldPoint.getIndex0()` and `getIndex1()` are the compound child indices of the two bodies in a contact — the "which part was hit" that D07-S5.1's hit resolution is built on. Nothing in gdx-bullet's API names them that; they are `m_index0`/`m_index1`, set by `btCompoundCollisionAlgorithm` through `setShapeIdentifiersA/B`, and their meaning depends on what kind of shape the body has.

### DISC-016 — a skinned mesh's node transform is not its placement
`GltfReader` composes each node's own transform and ignores joint weights (DEC-035). For ten of the Eclipse's 171 meshes that is wrong by up to **2.65 m**: the whole rear-left corner, which the file places through its joint matrices. Read the way the reader reads it, the car has three wheels and a fourth floating over the front axle.

### DISC-017 — Bullet ray tests a big convex box to about 14 cm
`btCollisionWorld::rayTest` finds a convex shape with an iterative subsimplex cast, not an analytic intersection, and its accuracy degrades with the shape's size. Against the test scene's 120 × 2 × 800 m ground box each suspension ray came back with a contact point up to **0.14 m** above the surface, differently every tick. A `btStaticPlaneShape` at the same place is exact.

### DISC-018 — an object in a downloaded car is a material group, not a part
`syndicate_dissect` treated each imported object as an island. On both shipped cars an object is everything sharing one material, spanning the whole vehicle: one Eclipse object is the entire cabin, another is both headlights and both tail lights at once; the Stampede's `Window_Material1` object is all of its glass. True connected-component separation turns 171 objects into **6,830 shells** and 1,101 into **6,078**, in 16 seconds each.

### DISC-019 — one shipped car names its materials, the other does not
Labelling shells by material name plus a physical glass test covers **99.4% of the Stampede's triangles and 35.9% of the Eclipse's**. The Stampede's materials are called `…Window_Material1` and `…CallipersCalliperA_Zone…`; the Eclipse's are called `bw00.001`, `bwfmp`, `bwfgd` and `oyctp`. Any segmentation design that relies on names works on one of the two cars this project ships.

### DISC-020 — assets/ was not a test input, so content changes never re-ran the tests
`assets/` is on no source set, so Gradle had no reason to believe it affects `test`. Regenerating the shipped meshes left every test task `UP-TO-DATE`, and the suite reported green against the meshes it had seen on some earlier run. CI, which has no such cache, failed six tests on the same commit.

### DISC-021 — a guardrail reading project.rootDir fails only when it finds a violation
`HeadlessSafetyCheckTask` formatted its violation paths with `file.relativeToOrSelf(project.rootDir)`. With the configuration cache on, reading `project` at execution time is a build failure — and that line only runs when the task has a violation to report, so the defect would have surfaced for the first time on the commit that broke the rule the task exists to guard.

### DISC-022 — a same-tick event is consumed before presentation runs, so every hit was silent
`DamageEvent` was published with `emitSameTick`, which `DamageSystem` (12) drains within the tick. PRESENT systems run after the tick, on the deferred bus, so slots 24 and 25 never saw a single hit: cars took damage with no spark and no noise, and the unit tests for both systems passed because they emitted their own events.

### DISC-023 — a crossfade removes a loop's value discontinuity and hides its phase one
`Waveform.loopable(seconds)` trimmed a crossfade measured in *seconds* off a buffer whose length had been chosen to hold a whole number of *cycles*, destroying exactly the property the caller established. Three of the six shipped engine loops — I4, V8 and V10 — held a fractional number of cycles and warbled once per pass. The existing loop test was green on all six, because a crossfade guarantees continuity in value and says nothing about phase.

### DISC-024 — jitpack.io is blocked by the sandbox proxy, so game-client cannot be built here
`game-client` cannot be compiled in the remote agent sandbox: its one JitPack-only dependency, `gdx-gltf` (DEV-001), is denied by the egress proxy with a 403 on CONNECT, and the artifact is not in the Gradle cache. Every other module builds and tests normally. The workaround is to compile the touched package directly with `javac` against the cached jars, which type-checks the code without resolving the unreachable dependency.

### DISC-025 — a parallel band-pass bank with no dry path is a 25 dB gate, not an exhaust
`SoundSynth.resonate` summed three band-pass filters and emitted only their output. Everything between the formants was attenuated by the sum of their skirts *and* by phase cancellation between them. For the 105 / 560 / 1750 Hz set that put a 25 dB hole at 267 Hz — which is a V8's firing frequency at the 4,000 rpm reference. The loudest component of the shipped `engine_loop_v8.wav` was 100 Hz.

### DISC-026 — measured against real engines, the synthesiser was too dark and far too clean
Thirty-nine CC-licensed engine recordings give two numbers to tune against: real exhaust harmonics fall at about **−7 dB per octave** from 100 Hz to 4 kHz, and stand **+9 to +17 dB** above the floor between them. `EngineSynth` measured **−22 dB/oct** and **+37 dB** — muffled, and almost noiseless. The second is what makes a synthesised engine sound synthesised.

### DISC-027 — an engine's periodic parts must all be tied to the crank, or it reads as a fault
The ignition was reported as sounding like "an audio error". Three separate constants in it were free of the engine: the crank-speed modulation ran at a hardcoded 6 Hz, the starter's gear whine held a fixed pitch, and a cranking engine made almost no exhaust noise at all. Tied to the crank, the same code produces a growl that differs per arrangement.

### DISC-028 — a real V8 recording says the even orders were 14 dB light, and why
The user supplied a Ford Mustang GT startup recording. Measured against it at matched engine speed, this synthesiser's odd sub-orders were within 7 dB and its **even** sub-orders 14 dB light. The cause is that two banks which are exact time-reverses of each other cancel their even content, and nothing in the model broke that symmetry. Fixed per-cylinder trims do; random jitter cannot.

### DISC-029 — the ignition timing was wrong in every dimension, against a real start
The same Mustang GT recording carries a complete ignition, and the model's timing was wrong in all three of its parts: the crank was too long, the catch was a ramp where the real one is a step, and the flare collapsed in a single frame instead of holding and decaying.

### DISC-030 — the rumble is an envelope ratio, and the sub-orders were never the problem
An engine's lope is the ratio of sub-order to firing-order *envelope* modulation. A real cross-plane V8 idling measures 17.4 and 18.2 on two steady windows. This synthesiser measured 1.25 — and the reason was not weak sub-orders. Those were already right.

### DISC-031 — fusing exhaust pulses with a comb re-creates the defect it is fixing
Successive blowdowns have to overlap for an engine to rumble rather than pump (DISC-030). The obvious mechanism — delay lines with the reflections mixed back — pins the spectrum, which is exactly the DISC-025 defect this whole rewrite exists to remove. An all-pass chain cannot.

### DISC-032 — a clip of several cars was used as a single-engine reference
A supplied recording of multiple cars starting was measured as though it were one four-cylinder at 1,450 rpm, giving a rumble ratio of 8.4. That number was then chased through a parameter search for most of a session. Its envelope-modulation peaks form no coherent order series at all.

### DISC-033 — a matching sub-order total hid a lope in the wrong place
DISC-030 fixed the ratio of sub-order to firing-order modulation and the user still reported no feelable rumble. They were right. The total was correct and every percentage point of it was at the wrong order.

### DISC-034 — the overrun crackle ran off a clock and sounded like popcorn
`CRACKLE_RATE_HZ = 26.0` produced 11 to 14 evenly spaced bangs a second on every arrangement at every engine speed. It is the same fault as the hardcoded 6 Hz crank in R38a5, introduced two sessions after that one was recorded, by the same hand.

### DISC-035 — `volume × density` is the wrong mass rule for vehicle art, by 25×
D09-R16 makes a part's mass `volume × density`, and G7's conservation across a fracture follows from it. Applied to a car's panels it is not imprecise but wrong in kind: a door skin either encloses a tenth of a cubic metre of air (785 kg of "steel") or encloses nothing (0 kg). Neither is within an order of magnitude of the 29 kg a door weighs.

### DISC-036 — a bearing of exactly zero lands in the last sector, not the first
`angular_coverage_deg` bins a vertex by `degrees(atan2(dy, dz)) % 360`. A vertex sitting exactly on the axle's `+z` bearing has `dy = 0` — except that the axle centre it is measured against is derived from a bounding box, so `dy` comes out as `-2.8e-17`, the bearing as `-1.8e-14`, and the modulo sends it to **359.99999…**, which is the twenty-fourth sector rather than the first.

### DISC-037 — a material name defined an axle, and took a third of the car with it
The Eclipse carries a flat bracket, 0.35 × 0.10 m and round to 0.29, whose material is called `vehicle_generic_smallspecmap_WHEEL`. C3 matches the whole token `wheel` and labels it one. As a **seed** for a wheel corner it dragged the front axle 0.37 m rearward, inflated the wheel to 1.44 m across, captured 891 shells — 37% of the car — and exported every one of them as brake furniture.

### DISC-038 — real art's topology decides what every later stage can do
Welding doubled vertices more than **halves** the shell count on both shipped cars — the Eclipse goes from 6,830 shells to 2,878 — and dissolving slivers is what makes damage morphs possible at all: with 502 degenerate faces removed the chassis could not be dented, and with 11,988 removed it dents at the second amplitude tried.

### DISC-039 — the fracture tool cannot cut a windscreen, and says so seven different ways
Every `glass` part on both shipped cars failed to fracture. The D09 tool is sound and its self-verification is what caught each failure, but a solidified 5 mm curved windscreen is not the kind of solid its Voronoi path was built and fixture-tested against, and it fails on three different guards depending on the pane.

### DISC-040 — our convex hull is not convex when the points are a slab of glass
`convex_hull` returned face sets that were not polyhedra at all on glass shards — 30 vertices carrying 71 triangles where Euler allows 56, with four of the hull's **own vertices** lying 22 mm outside it. The pipeline saw this only as a pane that would not fracture (exit 71, "hull does not enclose its source"), three stages away from the cause.

### DISC-041 — a model with no common parent lost 30 of its 31 objects before stage 1
`drop_foreign_roots` kept the single root subtree holding the most **objects**. Both shipped cars arrive as one parented hierarchy, so that kept the car. A scene of unparented meshes gives every root a count of one, the tie went to iteration order, and the pipeline deleted all but one object — then measured the survivor and reported a 2.9 m vehicle with a straight face.

### DISC-042 — a tank classifies as one rigid chassis, and the corner model is why
Run against a tank, the pipeline exits 0 and produces a **single rigid chassis part**: no wheels, no turret, no tracks, nothing hinged, 5,218 kg in one piece. The cue ensemble is not what fails — it labels all sixteen road wheels correctly at high confidence — and then the four-corner wheel model throws them away.

### DISC-043 — thickening a curved patch inflates it, so area is not recoverable from volume
The shell fracture's coverage check recovered each patch's area from its *solidified* volume and held the result to 1%. Four of nine panes on the shipped cars failed it — not because the cut was wrong, but because `solidify` offsets along vertex normals and a patch of convex curvature therefore encloses more than `its own area × thickness`.

### DISC-044 — a fixed windward fraction makes the dune's angle an output, and it was a ramp
D16-S5.3 specified the dune profile with a fixed windward fraction of 0.72, which makes the slip face's **angle** a consequence of dune height and wavelength rather than an input. At the parameters the document shipped — 9 m over 90 m — that angle is **19.6°**, well under the 25° a vehicle can climb. Every dune in the desert biome would have been a ramp on both sides.

### DISC-045 — dunes that all stand at repose partition the arena into parallel corridors
Making every dune slip face a wall (DISC-044) worked, and made the arena unplayable. Dunes run transverse to the wind, so uniformly non-zero dune heights produce uniformly continuous **walls**: the field measured 73% drivable and split into **42 disconnected regions**, the largest of them 23.8% of the arena. The connectivity check refused to load it, which is the only reason this was found at build time rather than by driving.

### DISC-046 — game-client builds, tests and *runs* in the sandbox, under xvfb
`game-client` compiles, its tests run, and the real client renders and writes a PNG — all inside the development sandbox. DISC-024 recorded that none of this was possible because JitPack was blocked; JitPack now resolves, and `xvfb-run` supplies the display the client was assumed not to have. The project's largest standing structural risk is gone.

### DISC-047 — fbm never reaches ±1, and the PBR environment brightens a flat colour fourfold
Two measured constants, both found by looking at output rather than by reading code, and both of which silently produced a plausible-looking wrong result. `TerrainNoise.fbm` is normalised to [-1, 1] and in practice spans about ±0.44. `RenderEnvironment` renders a plain base colour roughly four times brighter than the colour itself.

### DISC-048 — a base-colour socket behind a texture is a factor, not a colour
A Principled BSDF's Base Color socket means two different things. Unconnected it is the surface's colour. Connected to an image it is the `baseColorFactor` the texture is **multiplied by** — and writing a style band's value ceiling into it renders the whole vehicle at a fraction of its brightness.

### DISC-049 — a car does not stand on fully extended springs
`syndicate_prepare` placed each wheel slot at `axle + suspensionRestLengthM`, which is where the suspension is fully **extended**. No vehicle stands there. Both prepared cars settled 8.2 cm into the road — exactly their static sag — and the model's own ground plane stopped meaning anything.

### DISC-050 — a wheel measured from the shells that voted for it is not where the wheel is
A wheel part's origin came from the corner model's `axle` — the bounding box of whichever shells voted "wheel". That set spans brake furniture as well. On the Stampede it sat 3.6 cm above and 3 cm outboard of the wheel's actual centre, so every wheel spun about a point off its own middle and the car's track came out 6 cm wide of the real one.

### DISC-051 — the style pass painted 41 of 60 materials as trim, and erased the wheels
`classify` sent every material with no physical or nominal evidence to the **trim** row — near-black, non-metallic, rough. On the shipped Eclipse that is 41 of its 60 materials, both alloy wheels among them. The car rendered as grey mush with two black discs where its wheels are.

### DISC-052 — gdx-gltf can be built from source when JitPack is blocked
`game-client`'s one JitPack-only dependency is 135 Java files depending on nothing but `com.badlogicgames.gdx:gdx`, which is on Maven Central. When the egress proxy denies jitpack.io, the module can be cloned from GitHub, compiled with `javac`, and served from a directory repository added by a Gradle `--init-script`. No file in the repository changes.

### DISC-053 — `image.copy()` does not copy the pixels, and reports pure white
Measuring a texture's mean brightness by copying the image, scaling the copy to a thumbnail and reading its pixels returns **1.0 for every image on both shipped cars**. The copy is lazy; scaling something with no pixel data fills it white. `foreach_get` on the original reports what is in the file — and the real values span 0.006 to 0.96.

### DISC-054 — the garage drew every wheel a rest length up inside its own arch
`GaragePreview` places each part at its slot's `localTransform`. For a wheel that transform is the **suspension connection point**, not the axle — so every wheel was drawn 22 cm high (30 cm before DISC-049), tucked into the fender, and the car looked like it was sitting on its floor pan.

### DISC-055 — the last file you change is the one you never re-check
CI failed stage 1 on `INDEX.md: differs from a freshly generated index (D13-R8)`. The cause was not a forgotten command — `regenerateIndex` had been run — but its **position in the sequence**: the session summary was written after it, and a session summary is an index entry. The verification ran against a tree that no longer existed by the time it was committed.

### DISC-056 — the Actions API status you read can be an hour stale
While watching the run that fixed DISC-055, the `mcp__github__actions_*` responses reported the last stage `in_progress` for the better part of an hour. The job had actually finished green in **2m14s**. The stall was in the API cache, not on the runner, and the run was nearly cancelled because of it.

### DISC-057 — a downloaded model's scale is unknown, so every metre threshold measures nothing
Both shipped weapon models import into Blender at **100×**. Every absolute threshold applied to them before that is corrected therefore measures the wrong thing — and the failure is silent, because each stage produces a plausible-looking result on the way to a wrong one. The cannon separated into **203 shells instead of 22**, its bore axis was fitted to rivets, and it classified as a `LASER` weighing 631 kg.

### DISC-058 — a seam budget tests the artist; what the pipeline owes is contact
D17's seam rule was first written as a **gap budget**: after export, the distance between parent and child must be under 2 mm or the tool fails. It failed every weapon, and the reason is that the assertion was about the wrong party. Real art models clearances — a barrel inside its shroud, a sight above its rail — and a gap is the artist's decision, not the pipeline's defect.

### DISC-059 — a bot at its standoff distance was shuffling, not driving
Fitting the shipped vehicles with weapons turned `HeadlessMatchTest.everyBotDrives` red: the armed Stampede covered 18.4 m against a 20 m threshold while its opponents covered 118 m. The cannon was not the cause. A bot already at engagement range was being handed a destination a couple of metres from where it stood — inside its own turning circle — and it ground back and forth at walking pace for the entire match.

### DISC-060 — the cannon is a pedestal gun, and its cogs turn across the bore
`steampunk_cannon.glb` was read as a field piece on a wheeled carriage, and D17-R27 was written to discard the carriage as scenery. It is not a carriage. It is a **pedestal deck gun**: a square base plate, a column, and two toothed cogs that elevate the barrel. The rule threw away the mount and both gears — the parts most worth keeping — and no cue would have caught it, because the geometry that identifies a cog reads as noise in the bore plane.

The asset half of the project is functional: the Blender tool fractures a mesh and verifies its own output, the harness re-verifies that output inside Bullet, and the visual mode renders the destruction and captures it. `game-core` has its first system: `PhysicsSystem` in schedule slot 10, stepping a real `PhysicsWorld` at a fixed `TICK_DT` and mirroring Bullet's state into `Transform` and `Velocity`. `EntityDestroySystem` (slot 27, CLEANUP phase) is implemented. It safely tears down entities that were queued for destruction. ### DEV-001 — gdx-gltf 3.x does not exist; the pinned version is 2.3.0 from JitPack
D02-S4.1 (D02-R1) named "gdx-gltf 3.x" as the runtime glTF importer. No 3.x release exists; the newest is 2.3.0, and gdx-gltf is published to JitPack only, never to Maven Central. The catalog pins 2.3.0 and D02-S4.1 was amended in the same commit.

### DEV-002 — Cell Fracture is unavailable; cells built by half-space clipping
D09-S5.2 drives Blender's Cell Fracture add-on (`bpy.ops.object.add_fracture_cell_objects`), which wraps voro++. That add-on is not part of Blender 4.2's core distribution — it moved to the extensions platform — and is absent from both a stock install and the `bpy` PyPI module. Cells are constructed directly instead, by clipping a copy of the source mesh with the perpendicular-bisector half-spaces between sites.

### DEV-003 — Hull simplification maximises remaining volume, not minimum volume increase
D09-S5.5 says hull simplification repeatedly drops the vertex whose removal *increases* the hull volume least. That rule is correct for simplifying a concave outline and wrong for a convex hull, where removing any vertex can only shrink it — so as written it selects the single most destructive removal at every step. The implementation maximises the remaining volume instead, then re-inflates to restore enclosure.

### DEV-004 — test_complex_hollow does not fracture; the fixture is unprocessed
**Resolved 2026-08-08 by convex decomposition of the source (DEC-011).** Four of the five D14-S7.1 fixtures process to exit 0. `test_complex_hollow` does not: the shard mass sum comes out at 13571 kg against a true part mass of 2127 kg — 6.4x — so the tool correctly refuses it with `MASS_IMPLAUSIBLE` (72). The fixture is excluded from `:blender-tool:processFixtures` until the cause is fixed. It is now included and processes to exit 0: 14 shards summing to 2127.2 kg, 0.006% from the part mass.

### DEV-005 — cells on curved sources are not true Voronoi cells
**Resolved 2026-08-08 by the exact half-space path.** The bisect-and-fill cell construction of DEV-002 produced exact Voronoi cells on a planar-faced source and materially wrong ones on a curved source: on `test_sphere_r0.5`, 178 of 2400 sampled interior points were closer to a foreign site than to their own cell's, median depth 5.5% of the part's extent. Cells are now built as exact convex polytopes from half-spaces, and all three Voronoi properties hold on every convex fixture including a 100-shard case.

### DEV-006 — test_complex_hollow has no internal cross rib and weighs 2127 kg, not 2500
D14-R20 describes `test_complex_hollow` as a hollow 1 m box with 0.05 m walls **and one internal cross rib**, at ≈ 2500 kg. The checked-in fixture is the shell alone: no rib, 0.2710 m³, 2127.4 kg at steel density. The gap predates this session and was never recorded.

### DEV-007 — TestWorld builds its own Bullet world at 0.005 m, not via PhysicsWorld.create
D14-R10 requires the harness to construct its Bullet world through `game-core`'s `PhysicsWorld.create()`, and D06-R13 fixes the convex collision margin at 0.01 m. `TestWorld` did neither: it built its own world at 0.005 m, on Bullet's default solver settings. **Resolved 2026-08-08** — it delegates to `PhysicsWorld`, and its margin constant is an alias of the game's.

### DEV-008 — btRaycastVehicle has no removeWheel; detach reindexes only the ECS side
D05-S5.5 step 2 calls `vehicleController.removeWheel(index)` when a wheel part detaches. gdx-bullet 1.14.2's `btRaycastVehicle` exposes `addWheel` and no removal of any kind, so `PartDetachment` performs the wheel-index re-densification D05-R24 requires on the ECS side and leaves the native controller untouched.

### DEV-009 — a hanging part gets no constraint unless it is already a separate body
D06-R21 gives a destroyed-but-not-yet-detached part a `btFixedConstraint` so it hangs by a thread and breaks under load. A constraint joins two rigid bodies, and an attached part is not one — it is geometry inside the vehicle's compound (D06-R20, DEC-004). `DetachSystem` therefore creates the constraint only for a part that already has its own body; for every other part the `HANGING_TICKS` delay alone is the hang.

### DEV-010 — the asset loader does not read collision meshes; they arrive through a seam
D08-S5.3's `AssetRegistry.load` calls `GltfLoader.loadNode` to get a part's collision geometry out of `mesh.glb`. `game-core` has no glTF reader and cannot have gdx-gltf's, so `AssetLoader` takes the mesh from a `CollisionMeshSource` supplied by its caller and reports A503 when that returns nothing.

### DEV-011 — the dedicated server runs its own loop; no libGDX Application is created
D03-S5.1 step 4 wraps the headless server in a `HeadlessApplication`. `ServerMain` sets `Gdx.files = new HeadlessFiles()` and runs `HeadlessLoop` on the main thread instead.

### DEV-012 — every body is added with DefaultFilter in its mask, beyond D06-S4.4's table
`PhysicsWorld.addBody(body, layer)` passes `layer.mask() | 1` to Bullet rather than `layer.mask()`. The extra bit is `btBroadphaseProxy::DefaultFilter`, without which a ray-cast wheel finds no ground on any surface (DISC-011).

### DEV-013 — art-source holds whole-vehicle glTF in a bucket D08-R1 does not name
`art-source/` gains a `vehicles/` directory holding two whole-car glTF models — body and wheels in one mesh — rather than the per-part `.blend` files D08-S4.1's tree is made of. They are source art that has not been split into parts yet, and there was nowhere in the specified layout to put them.

### DEV-014 — an arena's collision is generated from its bounds, not loaded from collision.glb
`ArenaFactory` builds an arena's floor and four walls as convex boxes derived from `boundsMin`, `boundsMax` and `groundY`. D08-S4.7 gives an arena an `assets.collision` glTF file; the shipped arena declares none, and nothing reads one.

### DEV-015 — a detached wheel's native slot is disarmed, and indices never renumber
Supersedes DEV-008. `btRaycastVehicle` still has no `removeWheel`, so a detached wheel keeps its slot in the native controller. That slot's stiffness, damping, maximum force, friction, engine force, brake and steering are set to zero, and the surviving wheels **keep** their indices rather than being re-densified as (D05-R24) requires.

### DEV-016 — the crank is twice as long as the recording it was measured from
R38a9 requires ignition timing to be measured rather than invented, and it was: a Ford Mustang GT cranks for about 0.54 s before catching. Reproduced faithfully, the user reported "a complete lack of a startup sound". The crank is now 1.12 s for an eight — roughly double the measured value — and the starter is louder and audibly labours on each compression.

### DEV-017 — a replay steps the whole physics world, not just the local vehicle's body
D10-S5.5 step 3 replays unacknowledged inputs with `world.stepPhysicsOnly(TICK_DT)` and the comment "only the local vehicle's body needs re-simulating". `ReconciliationSystem` steps the entire `btDiscreteDynamicsWorld` per replayed input and then restores every remote entity from the newest snapshot.

### DEV-018 — eight of the catalogue's messages have wire ids and no codec
`MessageType` holds all twenty-one messages of D10-S4.2 with their wire ids and channels reserved. `Messages` encodes thirteen of them. `ScoreUpdate`, `MatchPhase`, `ChatMessage`, `AdminCommand`, `Ping`, `Pong` and `SelectVehicle` are declared and not yet written or read.

### SESS-027 — the glass shatters, the doors are found, and the tank is answered
Three asks: fix the glass shatter, decide whether the Stampede has doors and find them, and say what happens to a tank. All nine glass panes on both cars now fracture into 24 shards with mass conserved exactly; both cars now yield two hinged doors; and a tank was built and run rather than reasoned about, which produced a clear answer and turned up a defect that had nothing to do with tanks.

### SESS-028 — the ground gets a specification
The user asked whether ground and sky could be generated at runtime — textured, sloped, hilly, natural enough that driving lines and positioning matter — starting with desert and tarmac, destructible structures to follow. The answer is yes, and this session wrote the contract: `docs/16_procedural_arena_generation.md`, a seventeenth blueprint. No implementation.

### SESS-029 — the ground exists
Stage 1 of four: the terrain generator, its collision shape, and the queries everything downstream asks it. A car can now drive on generated hills. Nothing draws them, no road is carved, no structure exists — stages 2, 3 and 4.

### SESS-030 — the first twenty-six sessions, consolidated
SESS-001 through SESS-026 are archived (D13-R2c) and replaced by this one entry. What they built is recorded, in state rather than in narrative, by the progress entries PROG-026 through PROG-033; what they decided and discovered is in `decisions/` and `discoveries/`, which are unchanged.

### SESS-031 — a game you can start, and a repository that says what it is
A review session that turned into a build session. The client now opens on a main menu, leads to a garage where the player picks a vehicle from the real art, and deploys into a match it can leave; the distribution runs from any working directory and there is a Windows packaging task. Alongside that: the memory system was consolidated, the roadmap rewritten as a sequential plan, and several claims that had stopped being true were corrected.

### SESS-032 — parts belong to their vehicles, and the roster looks like one game
Four asks, all delivered: vehicle parts move under the vehicle that owns them, the five-part chassis-and-wheels models are retired for the pipeline's real output, every source material is normalised into a house style before anything measures it, and the vehicle system is ready for weapons — built-in ones derived from a model's own geometry, modular ones fitted to hardpoints every vehicle now offers.

### SESS-033 — headlights, and looking at the cars
Three asks. `game-client` compiles and runs here after all — gdx-gltf was built from source because JitPack is blocked (DISC-052). Headlights cast real light and draw visible beams at night. And looking at the styled cars found a defect that every test had passed over (DISC-051).

### SESS-034 — a palette, a tone band, and a car that stops bottoming out
The house style is retargeted at Crossout's wasteland and gains the two rules that make a roster cohere rather than merely making each surface plausible: a six-hue palette snap and a global luminance band, both applied to every surface and neither scaled by strength (DEC-079). The garage's wheels sat a rest length too high and now do not (DISC-054). And the client's runbook is written down.

### SESS-035 — two guns, taken apart
The user supplied two weapon models and asked for the whole feature: separated like the vehicles are, parts that move, a tool that normalises style and fixes geometry, logical damage, impulse, slot restrictions, clean attachment, screenshots. An eighteenth blueprint (D17) was written, a third Blender tool built, and both weapons now ship fitted and photographed.
