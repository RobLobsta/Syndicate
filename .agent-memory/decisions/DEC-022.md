# DEC-022: wheel radius comes from the collision mesh; suspension length is a constant

**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/06_physics_simulation.md#D06-S5.5, docs/05_vehicle_part_system.md#D05-S4.5, docs/08_asset_pipeline.md#D08-S4.2

**Status:** superseded (by DEC-029)

## Summary
`btRaycastVehicle.addWheel` needs a wheel radius and a suspension rest length, and no blueprint
authors either. The radius is derived from the wheel's collision mesh — half the larger of its Y and
Z extents — and the rest length is a constant, `WHEEL_SUSPENSION_REST_LENGTH_M = 0.3`.

## Details
D05-S4.5's stat table carries `SUSPENSION_STIFFNESS` and `FRICTION_SLIP` and D08-R5's part schema
carries mass, hit points and armour, but neither has a wheel radius, a rest length, damping figures
or a roll influence. D06-S5.5 specifies the vehicle model and the control loop without listing
`addWheel`'s arguments.

**Radius is derived, not defaulted.** A ray-cast wheel casts from its connection point and places
the contact one radius short of it, so a wrong radius parks the vehicle in the ground or hovers it
above — visible immediately and attributed to physics rather than to content. The mesh already
answers the question exactly: the axle runs along the vehicle's X (`WHEEL_AXLE_LOCAL = (-1,0,0)`),
so the wheel's silhouette is its YZ extent and its radius is half the larger of the two. A part whose
art is right gets a wheel that is right, with nothing to author and nothing to keep in sync.

**Everything else is a constant with a named default**, and the two that D05-S4.5 does author are
read through `StatBlock.resolve(stat, default)` so content can override them per wheel:

| Constant | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `WHEEL_SUSPENSION_REST_LENGTH_M` | 0.3 m | Bullet's own vehicle demo; a car that visibly settles without floating |
| `WHEEL_MAX_SUSPENSION_TRAVEL_CM` | 30 | matched to the rest length |
| `WHEEL_MAX_SUSPENSION_FORCE_N` | 12 000 | caps the launch a bottomed-out spring can produce |
| `WHEEL_SUSPENSION_STIFFNESS` | 20 | default for `SUSPENSION_STIFFNESS` |
| `WHEEL_DAMPING_RELAXATION` / `_COMPRESSION` | 2.3 / 4.4 | Bullet demo values; relaxation below compression so a landing settles |
| `WHEEL_FRICTION_SLIP` | 10.5 | default for `FRICTION_SLIP` |
| `WHEEL_ROLL_INFLUENCE` | 0.1 | deliberately low — see below |

Roll influence is the one worth arguing about. A ray-cast vehicle with a high roll influence tips on
a hard corner, and a vehicle in this game routinely loses half its armour and finds its centre of
mass somewhere the author never put it. The two together produce a car that rolls for no visible
reason, which reads as a bug rather than as consequence.

## Rationale / Context
Without this, the next session tuning vehicle handling finds seven magic numbers in `VehicleFactory`
and no way to tell which were chosen and which were typed. It also records the gap itself: if wheel
geometry should be authored, D05-S4.5 and D08-R5 are what need amending, and the radius derivation
is what would be replaced.

## Impact
`game-core` `vehicle` (`VehicleFactory`). Affects D06-S5.5 and, if the gap is closed by authoring,
D05-S4.5's stat table and D08-R5's part schema.
