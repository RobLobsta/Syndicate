# DEC-015: Per-vehicle compounds and their child-index maps are owned by ShapeCache

**Date:** 2026-08-08
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/06_physics_simulation.md#D06-S5.3, docs/06_physics_simulation.md#D06-S4.3, docs/02_technical_architecture.md#D02-S5.7
**Status:** active

## Summary
A vehicle's `btCompoundShape` and its slot-path-to-child-index map live in a `VehicleCompound` registered in `ShapeCache` under the key `(assemblyId, COMPOUND, entityIndex)`, and are released by `EntityDestroySystem` when the vehicle dies. They are the one instance-scoped, mutable entry in a cache that is otherwise immutable and shared.

## Details
D06-R8 makes `ShapeCache` the owner of every shape and says shapes are "immutable, cached, and shared". A vehicle's compound is none of those three: parts leave it as they detach, so two vehicles of the same assembly diverge the moment either takes damage. D06-S5.3 also requires a `childIndexBySlotPath` map alongside it, rebuilt on every structural change (R14).

Neither has a home in the component catalogue. D04-S4.3 lists no field for a compound or its index map on `VehicleChassisComponent` or anywhere else, and AC-D04-1 closes the catalogue.

The options considered:

1. **Reach the compound through `body.getCollisionShape()` and derive the index map on demand.** The compound's children carry no labels, so "which child is slot X" is only derivable if the children stay in a known order — and `removeChildShapeByIndex` swaps the *last* child into the removed slot, which destroys any such order. Restoring it would mean rebuilding the whole compound on every detach, which contradicts R14's mechanism.
2. **Add a component.** Breaks AC-D04-1.
3. **Chosen:** keep both in `ShapeCache`, which already owns the native. `ShapeCache.buildVehicleCompound(entity, assemblyId, children)` registers one; `vehicleCompound(entity)` retrieves it; `releaseVehicleCompound(entity)` disposes it.

`VehicleCompound.removeChild` mirrors Bullet's swap in its own list, so the map is rebuilt *inside* the removal and is never observably stale — which is a stronger form of AC-D06-7 and makes D06-E5 unreachable rather than merely forbidden.

Release ordering matters: `EntityDestroySystem` calls `releaseVehicleCompound` **after** disposing the body that references the compound (D02-S5.7 rule 5), not during the queue expansion. The reverse order is a use-after-free Bullet reports several steps later.

The part hulls a compound references are *not* released with it. Those are shared by every instance of the same part type and live until `ShapeCache.dispose()`.

## Rationale / Context
A future session looking for the compound will look on the vehicle's components first, find nothing, and either add a field (breaking AC-D04-1) or cache a child index somewhere (the exact bug D06-R14 exists to prevent). This entry says where it is and why a cache of immutable shared shapes has one mutable per-instance entry in it.

## Impact
`game-core` `physics` (`ShapeCache`, `VehicleCompound`), `system` (`MassPropertySystem`, `EntityDestroySystem`), `vehicle` (`PartDetachment`). `EntityDestroySystem` gained an optional `ShapeCache` constructor argument; the single-argument constructor is retained for schedules with no shape cache.
