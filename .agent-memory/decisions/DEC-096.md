# DEC-096: hover trim engages only when a rotorcraft is off the ground

**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/06_physics_simulation.md#D06-S5.5, docs/04_entity_component_model.md#D04-S4.3.4, docs/03_runtime_modes.md#D03-S4.1

**Status:** active

## Summary
`RotorControl` trims neutral collective to a hover only while the aircraft is not touching static
geometry. On the ground the collective is a raw thrust command resting at the bottom of its range,
which is where a real machine's collective sits on a pad. This closes DISC-071, and it is the first
of the three candidates that entry listed.

## Details
Two mappings, selected by `PhysicsWorld.isTouchingStatic`:

| | full down | neutral | full up |
|---|---|---|---|
| airborne | `MIN_COLLECTIVE_FRACTION` | hover | 1.0 |
| on the ground | `MIN_COLLECTIVE_FRACTION` | `MIN_COLLECTIVE_FRACTION` | 1.0 |

The vertical damping term — height hold — is scaled by the same engagement, because a machine
sitting on its skids being shoved downward should not answer by making more thrust.

**Neutral on the ground has to be the bottom of the range, not the middle of it.** The first attempt
mapped the grounded stick linearly from `MIN_COLLECTIVE_FRACTION` to 1.0, which puts neutral at 62.5%
of maximum — 95% of the Kestrel's own weight, and still enough to skate it 34 m down a 12° slope. The
measurement is the only thing that distinguishes the two designs; both read as correct.

**A switch rather than a blend.** A helicopter cannot leave the ground until the parked mapping
already exceeds hover, so the only deflection a lift-off happens at is one where the two mappings
have converged to within about a fifth of maximum thrust; the vertical damping absorbs the step
within a second or two. A continuous blend would need a clearance measurement, and every way of
taking one is wrong for a body that is already resting (see DISC-072).

## Rationale / Context
ROADMAP §5 listed this as a decision for the user, between trimming only when airborne, spawning
aircraft already flying, and letting the collective rest down. The user asked for the game to be
stabilised and its half-finished features completed, and a helicopter that destroys itself when
parked is the defect on that list; the other two candidates sidestep the case or change how flying
feels rather than fixing it. Recorded here so the choice reads as a choice.

**Reversible in one place.** The whole decision is `isFlying(body) ? 1f : 0f` in
`RotorControl.collectiveFraction`. Letting the collective rest down in the air as well means
deleting the airborne column; spawning aircraft flying means restoring the trim and moving the
spawn.

## Impact
`game-core` `vehicle` (`RotorControl`), `physics` (`PhysicsWorld.isTouchingStatic`). New tests
`aHelicopterParkedOnASlopeStaysParked` and `aParkedHelicopterStillTakesOff`, both on a 12° gradient,
because the flat-ground test passes with the defect present. Verified in the client: the Kestrel is
3/3 parts and stationary at frame 60 and again at frame 360 of a match it was given no input in.
Flying behaviour above the ground is arithmetically unchanged.
