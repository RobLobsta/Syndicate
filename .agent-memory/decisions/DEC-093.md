# DEC-093: a rotor is found by rotational symmetry about its own thin axis

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S4.2, docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S5.4, docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S4.1

**Status:** active

## Summary
`syndicate_prepare` gains a `rotor` label, a C1 geometric cue that finds one, and two roles — `main`
and `tail` — keeping the two discs apart. The cue reuses D15-S5.4's rotational-symmetry machinery
rather than inventing a second measure, generalised off the x axis. DEC-094 covers the numbers
derived once a disc is found.

## Details
**The cue is three measurements, each excluding something different that is also flat.** A rotor is
flat (`flatness >= 0.90`), *square in the plane normal to its thin axis* (a new
`Shell.disc_aspect >= 0.80`), and at least 0.60 m across.

`disc_aspect` does the real work and did not exist. `roundness` asks the same question only about y
against z, because it was written for a wheel and a wheel is thin across the car; a main rotor is
thin *vertically*. Without it the Kestrel's tail-boom decal — **flatter** than its own tail rotor,
0.983 against 0.946 — is indistinguishable from a rotor. Footprint separates them: 0.42 against 1.00.

**Two sufficient symmetry tests, because a rotating piece has two shapes** — D15-R24a's distinction,
and this taxonomy holds one of each. The main rotor is a *pattern* (three blades, nine sectors) and
passes on symmetry order; the tail rotor is a *solid of revolution* covering 270° and passes on
coverage. Neither test alone finds both.

**The hub is the area centroid, never the box centre**, and it is what the feature turned on: from
the box centre the Kestrel's disc measures **symmetry order 1** and the cue finds nothing; from the
centroid it measures 3. The same point anchors the exported radius and the part origin (DISC-070).

**Main against tail is decided by the axis, not by size.** A main rotor turns about the vertical —
that is what makes its thrust lift — and an anti-torque rotor about a horizontal axis, because its
thrust pushes sideways. True of every rotorcraft with a tail boom.

## Rationale / Context
The concrete future failure this stops: the next aircraft's rotor is labelled `chassis`, as this one
was on the first run — C1 votes `chassis` at 0.7 for anything spanning a third of the vehicle, and a
main rotor spans most of it. Merged into the chassis it cannot be shot off, makes no lift, and takes
the collision hull out to the blade tips.

61% of the Kestrel's triangles stay `unclassified` and merge into the chassis, which D15-R2 makes a
first-class outcome: cabin, fin, stabilisers and skids have no label in a taxonomy written for cars.
Recorded rather than fixed — inventing four labels speculatively is how a closed taxonomy stops being
one.

## Impact
`blender-tool`: `labels.py` (the `rotor` row across eight tables), `shell.py` (`thinnest_axis`,
`disc_aspect`), `cues.py`, `roles.py`, `grouping.py` (an `axis` parameter on the symmetry functions,
defaulting to the old behaviour) and `manifest.py`. D15 gains R6b, R6c and R6d.
