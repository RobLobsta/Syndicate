# DEC-053: induction is a second voice, keyed on a closed set like the configuration is

**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S8, docs/05_vehicle_part_system.md#D05-S5.6

**Status:** active

## Summary
A new `Induction` enum — `NATURALLY_ASPIRATED`, `TURBO`, `SUPERCHARGED` — joins `EngineConfiguration` on `EngineVoice`. The bank holds one loop per forced member plus a release for the one that has one; the runtime derives pitch and gain per vehicle. D15-S8 was amended in the same commit (R36a–c).

## Details
Both shipped cars are forced-induction and neither sounded like it. Before this session, a grep for turbo or supercharger found the words only in Java comments describing the reference cars — nothing in the synthesiser, nothing in the playback path.

Induction follows the D15-R37 bargain exactly as configuration does: a closed set of three, one asset per forced member, nothing per vehicle. Three files — `induction_loop_turbo`, `induction_loop_supercharged`, `induction_release_turbo`.

**The two forced types differ structurally, not by tuning**, and that is R36b. A supercharger is geared to the crank: its whine is a hard tone at a fixed order of engine speed (`driveRatio` 9.6, giving 640 Hz at 4,000 rpm) and it is present whenever the engine turns. A turbo is spun by exhaust flow: it is broadband rush (`tonality` 0.35) that needs revs *and* load, so `inductionGainAt` multiplies `revFraction²` by throttle for a turbo and adds them for a blower. That multiplication is the off-boost hole, and it is most of what makes a turbo car sound like one.

Only `TURBO` has a release: a geared blower has nothing to let go of. `shouldRelease` needs both a throttle transition and a rev floor — without the floor a car blows off every time it rolls to a halt, the most recognisable way to make a turbo sound like a toy.

## Rationale / Context
The concrete future failure: a session adds a naturally aspirated vehicle, finds two cars that already sound different, and assumes configuration alone carries that difference. It does not — the pair differs in cylinders, rev range, power *and* induction, and dropping the last returns them to one exhaust note at two pitches. `theShippedPairBreathesDifferently` says so.

The second trap is treating turbo and supercharger as one sound at two settings. They are separated by one parameter (`tonality`) precisely so the separation is cheap; economising by deleting a member gives a supercharged car that whooshes.

## Impact
- `shared-models`: new `Induction`; `AudioEvent` gains `INDUCTION_LOOP`, `INDUCTION_RELEASE`.
- `game-core`: `EngineVoice` gains `induction` and six methods; `VehicleProfile` gains the field and its builder arg; `VehicleProfiles` marks the Eclipse `TURBO` and the Stampede `SUPERCHARGED`.
- `asset-pipeline`: `SoundSynth.inductionLoop` / `inductionRelease`; `SoundBankBuilder.buildInduction`.
- `game-client` `AudioSystem`: a second looping voice per vehicle and a release one-shot on lift.
- `docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S8`: R36 table extended, R36a and R36b added.
