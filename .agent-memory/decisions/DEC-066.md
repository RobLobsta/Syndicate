# DEC-066: a piece turns with a wheel if it covers the circle **or** repeats around it

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S5.4, docs/06_physics_simulation.md#D06-S5.5

**Status:** active

## Summary
DEC-044's angular-coverage test gains a second, independently sufficient one: a material group in a
wheel corner also rotates if its occupied sectors are invariant under a turn of `360/n` for some
`n ≥ 3`. Neither test replaces the other; a piece rotates if it passes either.

## Details
Coverage answers "is this a solid of revolution?" — a tyre, a rim, a brake disc all occupy 360°, a
caliper occupies 90–150°, and the gap is wide (DEC-044). It does not answer "is this a **pattern**
that repeats around the axle?", and D15-R22's own worked example is exactly that case: five lug
nuts occupy five sectors out of twenty-four, so coverage calls them 75° and sends them to the hub,
while the set is plainly invariant under a fifth of a turn.

R22 papers over this by making the *material* the unit of judgement, on the reasoning that a lug nut
shares its material with the rim it is screwed into. On the shipped cars that happens to hold. It is
a proxy for the property, not the property, and it fails the moment an artist gives the fasteners
their own material — which is a normal thing to do.

`MIN_SYMMETRY_ORDER` is 3. Two pieces opposite each other are symmetric under half a turn and are
just as likely to be the two ends of one bracket, and no wheel is fastened with fewer than three
studs.

## Rationale / Context
The failure this prevents is quiet and visible only in motion: a rim that loses its lug nuts, spoke
details and valve stem to the chassis still looks right standing still, still reports the correct
diameter, and sheds its fasteners into mid-air the moment the wheel turns. It is also the shape of
bug that gets "fixed" by lowering `ROTATION_SYMMETRY_MIN_DEG`, which would let a 150° caliper
through and put a brake caliper in orbit.

## Impact
- `grouping.rotational_symmetry_order` and `grouping.occupied_sectors`; `roles.resolve_rotation`
  accepts either test.
- D15-R24a records the rule and D15-R24b the numerical trap beside it (DISC-036).
- The corner block of the report carries `symmetryOrder` beside `coverageDeg`, so which of the two
  tests carried a group is visible rather than inferred.
