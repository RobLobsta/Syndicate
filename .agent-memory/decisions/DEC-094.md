# DEC-094: a rotor's thrust is disc loading, its speed is tip speed

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S5.8, docs/08_asset_pipeline.md#D08-S4.2, docs/05_vehicle_part_system.md#D05-S4.5

**Status:** active

## Summary
Once DEC-093's cue has found a disc, every number the runtime needs is derived from its geometry
rather than authored: thrust from disc area, governed rpm from a constant blade-tip speed, blade
count from the symmetry order already measured, and radius from the furthest blade tip from the hub.
A **tail** rotor is the one exception and is sized against the main rotor instead.

## Details
**Thrust is disc area times a disc loading of 320 N/m².** That makes a bigger rotor lift more without
anyone authoring it, and it is how a real rotor's lift scales — disc loading for a light helicopter
runs 250–400 N/m². The Kestrel's disc gives 22.4 kN, 1.43 times its weight, which is the margin a
real machine works at.

**Governed rpm is a constant 210 m/s tip speed divided by the radius.** Blade tips must stay below
about Mach 0.65 or the retreating blade stalls, so a bigger disc must turn slower and tip speed is
near-constant across the class. That gives 424 rpm on a 4.72 m blade.

**A tail rotor is sized as a fraction of the main rotor**, 0.11, which is why `rotor_thrust_n` needs
the main figure passed in. Disc loading on a 0.5 m fan gives about 250 N, nowhere near enough to trim
a 22 kN disc's torque, because **a tail rotor is not sized to lift anything.** It produces a
*moment*, and what it is a fraction of is the thing it opposes.

**Thrust is the stat `rotorThrustN`, not a field on the `rotor` block**, so degradation reaches it
(DEC-090) — a camelCase key with an `{"add": …}` term like every other stat. The first cut wrote a
bare number under a `SCREAMING_CASE` key, which `AssetLoader` rejected as A206 and which left the
aircraft grounded with a rotor that turned and lifted nothing. The loader said so; nobody read the
findings.

## Rationale / Context
The concrete future failure this stops: a second helicopter with a 12 m rotor flies identically to
this one, because the numbers were the Kestrel's copied into a table rather than derived. Every
constant here is a *physical* property of rotors — disc loading, tip speed, the ratio a tail rotor
bears to a main one — so a different disc gives different numbers for the right reason.

The radius is measured from the hub rather than as half the bounding box, and that is not a detail:
the box gives 4.10 m against a true swept 4.724 m, and thrust goes as the square of it, so the box
reading would have cost a third of the aircraft's lift (DISC-070).

## Impact
`blender-tool/syndicate_prepare/manifest.py` — `rotor_block`, `rotor_thrust_n`, `_disc_radius_m` and
the three constants — and `prepare.py`, which computes the main rotor's thrust first so a tail rotor
can be sized against it.
