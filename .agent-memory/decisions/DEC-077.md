# DEC-077: a built-in weapon is derived, a modular one is authored

**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S5.10, docs/08_asset_pipeline.md#D08-S4.2, docs/05_vehicle_part_system.md#D05-S4.3

**Status:** active

## Summary
Weapons arrive two ways and the project now expresses both. A **built-in** weapon is geometry the
model came with — a tank's barrel — labelled `weapon` by the preparation pipeline, exported as a
`WEAPON` part under its own vehicle, with a `weapon` block derived from its shape. A **modular**
weapon or utility module is authored content in the shared library and fits any vehicle, because
every prepared vehicle now offers five mounting points its own art never supplied.

## Details
**The loader reads the `weapon` block.** D08-R6 has specified it since the beginning and nothing
parsed it: a part declaring `"category": "WEAPON"` loaded as an inert lump, and `WeaponSystem` could
only ever fire a weapon a test had constructed in Java. Four new rules go with it — A216 (a block on
a non-weapon, or a weapon with none), A217 (an unknown family, or `RAM`, which is a chassis property).

**Modules get the same shape.** `ModuleFamily` (RADAR, AMMO_FEED, RADIATOR, REINFORCER, CLOAK,
BOOSTER, SMOKE, REPAIR) and `ModuleBlock` (family, charges, radius), with A218 and A219. The split
that matters is passive versus active: a radiator's whole effect is its `heatPerShot` stat and it
needs no block at all, where a cloak has a state — idle, running, recharging — that no stat
represents. Its numbers are two new stats, `moduleDurationS` and `moduleCooldownS`, so a damaged
cloak stays lit for less time exactly as a damaged weapon fires slower (the DEC-039 argument).

**Hardpoints are derived from the body box.** One `TURRET_MOUNT` on the roof and four `HARDPOINT`s —
bonnet, tail, each flank — placed as fractions of half width, height and length-from-the-rear, so
they transfer from a hatchback to a tank. Capacity is 8% of kerb mass with a 40 kg floor. They are
declared on the chassis and filled by nobody, and every check that reports an unfilled slot exempts
them **by id**, not by slot type: a brake hub also occupies a `HARDPOINT`-typed slot (DEC-063).

**A built-in weapon's block is derived from two things only**, because they are the two the geometry
determines: the family, from aspect ratio (a barrel four times longer than it is wide is a cannon,
anything shorter an autocannon), and the muzzle, at the part's forward extent.

## Rationale / Context
Without hardpoints, shipping a modular weapon would mean re-cutting every vehicle to add a slot for
it — which makes "modular" a promise the content cannot keep. Without the loader reading the block,
authoring one would produce a part that loads, validates, and cannot fire, with nothing saying why.

## Impact
- Both shipped vehicles offer five mounting points, listed in their `manifest.json`.
- `assets/parts/` is ready for weapon and module content and contains none yet.
- No runtime behaviour for active modules exists: the data contract lands here, the systems follow
  the content.
