# DEC-044: what turns with a wheel is decided by rotational symmetry

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S5.4, docs/06_physics_simulation.md#D06-S5.5

**Status:** active

## Summary
A piece captured into a wheel corner rotates with the wheel only if its geometry occupies at least
`ROTATION_SYMMETRY_MIN_DEG` (300°) of the circle about the axle, measured over vertices in 15°
sectors. Everything else — the brake caliper — is returned to the chassis.

## Details

**The bug.** The caliper was exported inside the wheel and spun with it, which is visible in any
capture and invisible to every measurement: the wheel's diameter, width and axle position are taken
from the round seed islands alone, so the caliper never disturbed a number.

**Why not names.** `callip` matches on both cars, but a name-driven rule also needs a whitelist of
what *does* rotate, and the Eclipse's rim is `material_51` and `oyctp`. That whitelist is unbounded
(DISC-019).

**The physical test.** A part bolted to a rotating wheel must be rotationally symmetric about the
axle or it would sweep through the bodywork once a revolution. Measured on both cars: every rotating
piece covers 360°; calipers cover 90–150°.

**Three implementation details that are not optional.**
- Coverage is measured from **vertices**, not bounding-box corners. A five-spoke rim's box lands in
  four sectors, so a box-based measure cannot tell a spoked wheel from a caliper.
- The unit of judgement is a **material group within a corner**, never a single island. A lug nut
  occupies 15° and plainly rotates; what it lacks is not size but a partner to be rotated onto, and
  its material is the best proxy for "the same kind of part". Judged island by island, the Stampede
  lost its lug nuts, spoke details and valve stem — 177 shells of one rim material — which shrank the
  exported hull 1.2 mm and dropped tyre geometry onto the floor pan (DISC-020).
- The test covers **seed** islands too. `is_wheel_shaped` asks whether a silhouette is square, which
  a caliper bolt is; on the Stampede eight caliper fragments of 1–40 triangles seeded the front-right
  corner beside the 4,516-triangle rim.

**Where the caliper goes.** Back to the chassis — an approximation, since a caliper is unsprung and
should follow suspension travel while declining to rotate. The exact answer is a `hub` part in a
non-rotating slot at the axle, which D15-S4.1 defines and the part schema does not yet carry.

**Verified.** No caliper material appears in any of the four exported wheels; every wheel's diameter,
width and axle are unchanged to four decimal places, and the Stampede's wheel keeps all 8,721 of its
triangles.

## Rationale / Context
The general principle is worth more than the fix: when a semantic question ("is this a caliper?") has
a physical consequence ("does it rotate?"), test the consequence. It generalises across models, needs
no vocabulary, and cannot be defeated by an artist's naming.

## Impact
`blender-tool/syndicate_dissect/dissect.py` (`angular_coverage_deg`, `prune_non_rotating_seeds`,
`capture_wheel_furniture`, `WheelGroup.static`), the four `assets/parts/wheel_*/mesh.glb`, and
`assets/parts/chassis_*/mesh.glb`, which gained the caliper triangles.
