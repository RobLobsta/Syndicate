# DEC-054: bank divergence scales with how unevenly a bank fires

**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S8, docs/05_vehicle_part_system.md#D05-S5.6

**Status:** active

## Summary
`SoundSynth.bankDivergence` returns 0.10 for an arrangement whose banks fire identically and 1.0 for the cross-plane V8, interpolated on `bankFiringSpreadDegrees() / 180`. It scales both the second bank's formant detune and its manifold delay. A global constant does not work, and the two ways it fails are opposite.

## Details
A cross-plane V8's bank patterns are time-reverses of each other, so their spectra are conjugates: summed perfectly coherently the odd orders cancel to **exactly zero** and the engine is even at order 8. That is not a modelling artefact — it is why cross-connecting the banks of a real one audibly flattens it. The burble is therefore uneven firing *failing to cancel*, and what stops it cancelling is the two banks reaching the listener differently.

Two wrong answers preceded this one, and they bracket it.

**Too much.** 6% detune and a 1.6 ms delay on every V. At 4,000 rpm a V8's firing interval is 7.5 ms, so 1.6 ms is a fifth of the way to the next cylinder — it manufactured half-order content on arrangements that fire perfectly evenly. Measured, the V6 and V10 carried more off-firing-order energy than the V8: the engine that should be lumpiest was third of six.

**Too little.** Cut to a physically plausible 0.3 ms, which cancelled the V8's odd orders almost completely and left it one weak line, no richer than a V6's.

Measured on the shipped bank, referenced against each engine's own firing order rather than the global spectral peak: the V8 carries orders 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 and 15, with order 3 as its loudest line; V10 and V12 carry nothing below their firing order; the V6 keeps one order-3 line, which is the offbeat a real V6 has.

## Rationale / Context
The concrete future failure: someone tuning "more burble" raises the divergence constant, gets it on the V8, and silently makes every even-firing V lumpy — the exact fault the first attempt had, arrived at from the other direction. The parameter is not a volume knob on burble; it is the coupling between two banks, and only an arrangement with genuinely different bank patterns should be allowed much of it.

The measure that made this legible is the count of sub-firing-order *lines*, not their total energy. The V6 and V8 differ in kind — one offbeat against an odd-order series — and a total makes those look like the same thing at two volumes, which is what hid the first wrong answer.

## Impact
- `asset-pipeline` `SoundSynth`: `bankDivergence`, `BANK_DETUNE_MAX`, `BANK_DELAY_MAX_SECONDS`, `BANK_DIVERGENCE_MIN`.
- `SoundBankTest.aCrossPlaneV8BurblesAndAnEvenFiringVDoesNot` asserts the line counts, not a total.
- `docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S8` R37a2 records the cancellation argument.
