# DEC-095: neutral collective hovers, and it is a fourth input axis

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/04_entity_component_model.md#D04-S4.3.4, docs/06_physics_simulation.md#D06-S5.5, docs/03_runtime_modes.md#D03-S4.1

**Status:** active

## Summary
`PlayerInputComponent` gains a fourth axis, `collective`, and `RotorControl` trims it so that a
released stick **holds height** rather than holding a thrust. Two terms do that: the trim itself,
`weight / maxThrust`, and a damping term on vertical rate. DEC-090 covers the rotor being a part;
this covers how a person flies it.

## Details
**A fourth axis rather than a reuse.** A helicopter climbs and goes forward *at the same time*, and
those are one stick on a car — `throttle` is already the fore-aft cyclic and `brake` is already the
handbrake. Every vehicle carries the field and a wheeled one ignores it. The alternative, a second
input component, splits the family of every system that reads intent, from
`InputCollectionSystem` through replication to `ReconciliationSystem`.

Bound to **Space** and **left Ctrl**. Space doubles as the handbrake, which is safe because the two
meanings never coexist on one vehicle: a car has no collective and a helicopter has no handbrake.
`ScriptedSource` gained the same key so a capture can fly — DISC-051's principle, that a claim about
behaviour should be a photograph rather than an inference.

**Neutral hovers, and that is a design choice rather than a physical one.** The model trims the
collective to the fraction of maximum thrust that cancels weight and spends the axis either side of
it. Without that trim the aircraft is unflyable on a keyboard — a key is on or off, so a stick that
meant "thrust" would leave a player oscillating between climbing and falling with nothing in between.

**Cancelling weight is not holding height**, and the first build did only the first. A Kestrel
released from full collective at 80 m coasted another 72 m, because nothing opposed the climb it had
built — a balloon rather than an aircraft. A term proportional to vertical rate fixes it and gives
the climb a terminal rate for free, about 10 m/s here against an H125's 8.5.

## Rationale / Context
The concrete future failure this stops: somebody reads the hover trim as a physical claim and
"corrects" it to a plain thrust command on the grounds that a real collective does not self-trim.
It is deliberately arcade, and this entry exists so that is a decision rather than an accident.

**It has a known cost**, recorded in DISC-071: neutral collective is right in the air and wrong on
the ground, so a helicopter parked on a slope has 100% of the thrust it needs to fly and slides
until it destroys its own rotor. Fixing that is a follow-up and a feel question, and it is in
ROADMAP §5 for the user.

## Impact
`game-core` (`PlayerInputComponent.collective`, `RotorControl.collectiveFraction`,
`VERTICAL_DAMPING_PER_MPS`, `MIN_COLLECTIVE_FRACTION`, `MAX_HOVER_FRACTION`); `game-client`
(`KeyboardMouseSource`, `LibGdxDevices.Desk`, `ScriptedSource`). `RotorFlightTest` asserts hover,
climb and descent; `KestrelFlightTest` asserts the shipped aircraft climbs and then settles.
