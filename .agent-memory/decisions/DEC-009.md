# DEC-009: MAX_SHARDS_PER_PART raised from 64 to 256

**Date:** 2026-08-08
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/00_master_index.md#D00-S6.4, docs/09_blender_destruction_tool.md#D09-S4.2

**Status:** active

## Summary
The hard cap on shards per part goes from 64 to 256. The old value was chosen before there was a fracture implementation to measure; the exact half-space path makes a 100-shard fracture cost 2 seconds and hold every correctness property, so the cap was bounding nothing real.

## Details

**Measured on the exact path** (`test_cube_1m`, 1 m steel cube):

| Shards | Wall time | Part mass | Checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 1 s | 7850.0 kg | 31/31 |
| 100 | 2 s | 7850.0 kg | 31/31 |
| 200 | 4 s | 7850.0 kg | 31/31 |

`test_sphere_r0.5` at 100 shards takes 7 s — the sphere has 720 face planes to intersect against, and that dominates.

**What the new cap is bounding.** Not the tool, which scales roughly linearly. The real runtime limit is the debris budget: `MAX_DEBRIS_BODIES` is 256 (D00-S6.4), for the whole world. One 100-shard part is a third of it. So a high shard count is a *hero destruction* setting — a chassis, a boss part — not a default for every armour panel, and content review is where that judgement belongs rather than a constant that forbids it.

256 rather than unbounded because `A505` (D08-S5) validates manifests against it, and a cap that can never be hit is not a check.

## Rationale / Context
Raising a D00-S6.4 constant touches the tool, the game, and asset validation at once, and the obvious question later is "was 256 measured or guessed?". The table above is the answer, along with the reason the debris budget rather than the tool is what actually constrains the number.

## Impact
`shared-models` `SimulationConstants`, `blender-tool/syndicate_fracture/cli.py`, D00-S6.4, D09-S4.2, D09-E4. `MAX_DEBRIS_BODIES` is unchanged and is now the binding limit.
