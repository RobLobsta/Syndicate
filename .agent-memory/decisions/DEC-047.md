# DEC-047: an engine is keyed on its configuration, not on its vehicle's weight class

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S8, docs/05_vehicle_part_system.md#D05-S5.6

**Status:** active

## Summary
Engine loops are generated one per `EngineConfiguration` — I4, I6, V6, V8, V10, V12 — and a vehicle's own `EngineVoice` (configuration, idle rpm, redline rpm, peak power) decides the pitch, gain and low-end weight it is played at. The first cut keyed them on `light`/`medium`/`heavy` and was wrong.

## Details

D15-R37 says sounds are per class and per material, never per vehicle, and the reason is cost: a per-vehicle bank gates every new car on an audio pass. That rule is right. What was wrong was reading "class" as *vehicle* class.

`light`, `medium` and `heavy` describe how much a car weighs. What an engine sounds like is decided by how often it fires and how evenly — `cylinders × rpm / 120` for a four-stroke, plus the arrangement's firing evenness. Those are unrelated: two cars of the same weight with a V6 and a V8 sound nothing alike, and keying on weight made them identical.

Configuration is the right axis and keeps the set closed. Six values cover every car worth modelling. At the reference 4,000 rpm they fire at 133, 200, 200, 267, 333 and 400 Hz — I6 and V6 share a pitch and differ in roughness, which is the distinction a listener actually makes between them.

**Two cars sharing a configuration still differ**, and that is where the rest of the design sits. `EngineVoice` derives three things the runtime plays with:

- **Pitch** = `firingHz(rpm) / firingHz(4000)`, clamped to a factor of four — beyond that resampling stops sounding like an engine.
- **Gain**, rising with revs and with peak power on a saturating curve. Linear made the biggest engine deafening long before it was the fastest.
- **Low-end weight**, rising with power and cylinder count. The more convincing half of "powerful sounds powerful": a big engine is not a loud small engine, and the spectral difference survives a phone speaker where the volume difference does not.

**Peak power is the physics' own `enginePowerW`**, not a second number. A car that gets faster gets louder in the same commit, and no tuning pass can leave the two disagreeing.

The shipped pair: the Eclipse is a V6 to 8,000 rpm at 417 kW, the Stampede a V8 to 7,600 at 547 kW. Different cylinder count, different rev ceiling, 130 kW apart — audibly three different things at once.

## Rationale / Context
The obvious future move is per-vehicle engine samples, and this entry makes that a decision rather than a drift. The parametric model costs six files for the whole roster; a sampled one costs a file per car forever.

## Impact
`EngineConfiguration` in `shared-models`, `EngineVoice` in `game-core`, `VehicleProfile`'s new engine fields, the six `engine_loop_*` files, and D15-S8's R37a/R37b/R37c.
