# DEC-056: the engine is synthesised at runtime; 27 of the bank's 74 files are deleted

**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S8, docs/04_entity_component_model.md#D04-S4.4

**Status:** active

## Summary
`EngineSynth` in `game-client` integrates the crank angle forward at the simulation's rpm and fires cylinders as they come round, a block at a time from the audio callback. Six engine loops, eighteen start/stop/overrun one-shots and three induction files are deleted; the bank goes 74 → 47. `EngineVoice` loses its sound ids, its pitch ratio and `REFERENCE_RPM`; `AudioEvent` loses six members.

## Details
The prompt was DISC-025 — the shipped loops sounded at pitches unrelated to their engines — but fixing the filter would not have justified this on its own. Three things a file cannot do did.

**Condition.** A damaged engine misfires, drops a cylinder and loses its exhaust. All three change *when* a cylinder fires and *what happens to the pulse afterwards*, and resampling preserves neither. A dropped cylinder is one pulse not laid down; the even-order nulls of the bank's geometry filling in is the lope. Measured, the V8's even orders rise about 30 dB when cylinder 4 dies — nobody wrote that.

**Transients were assets only because a loop cannot change speed.** Twelve deleted files are `engine_start_*` and `engine_stop_*`, authored at a nominal 800 rpm idle neither shipped car has (they idle at 750 and 850). Cranking is 260 rpm with nothing catching; shutdown is the same engine running out of rotation. Both are now phases of `EngineRunState`.

**The reference rpm and its clamp are gone.** `pitchAt` clamped to [0.25, 4], and both shipped cars idle below 1,000 rpm — so below that the pitch stopped tracking revs at all and every car idled at the same note.

**Cost.** 186 ns/sample for one voice, 112× real time; 24 voices are 24% of one core. Impacts, shatters, debris, tyres, weapons and fire stay files — they are events and textures. An engine is neither, which is why it kept needing more of them.

## Rationale / Context
This does not weaken D15-R37's *"per class, never per vehicle"*: there is no per-vehicle asset because there is no asset. The risk taken is that engine audio moves from inspectable content into code, which is why T-D15-11 through T-D15-13 assert on output spectra rather than on parameters.

The overrun one-shot did not survive and did not need to — dropping the load *is* the overrun. What is lost is the crackle those files carried, a real gap named in PROG-022.

## Impact
- `assets/audio/`: 74 → 47 files; `AudioEvent` 15 → 9 members; `SoundSynth` loses ~370 lines.
- New in `game-client` `audio`: `EngineSynth`, `EngineRunState`, `EngineMixer`, `EngineAudioOutput`.
- `EngineVoice` keeps only what describes the engine: configuration, rev range, power, induction, plus `revFraction`, `gainAt` and `shouldRelease`.
- D15-R36 table shortened; R37a3 added; R37b and R37c amended to drop the pitch ratio and low-end weight.
- Supersedes the engine half of DEC-046 and PROG-021's bank inventory; DEC-052 and DEC-054's firing geometry carries over unchanged into `EngineSynth`.
