# DEC-064: a prepared vehicle is weighed from its footprint; the chassis takes the balance

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S5.8, docs/05_vehicle_part_system.md#D05-S5.7, docs/08_asset_pipeline.md#D08-S5.4

**Status:** superseded (by DEC-067)

## Summary
A dropped-in model has a kerb mass nobody measured and a power budget nobody authored. The pipeline
derives both: mass per part from `area × wall thickness × density` (DISC-035) with the **chassis
taking whatever is left** of a target derived from the vehicle's footprint, and `powerCost`
**distributed** across the parts so the assembly's budget equals its class's target exactly.

## Details
**Mass.** Every part but the chassis is weighed from its own geometry. The chassis is
`target − Σ(others)`. This is how the shipped Eclipse is authored — 1350 kg of chassis plus four
37.5 kg wheels making 1500 — and it means the vehicle hits a plausible kerb weight without anybody
knowing the wall thickness of the particular bonnet the artist modelled. The target is `--mass` when
given, and otherwise `175 kg/m²` over the footprint, which puts a 4.5 × 1.9 m car at 1497 kg and a
5.9 × 2.05 m pickup at 2118 kg. If the measured parts would leave the chassis under 45% of the
vehicle, the target is raised to keep it there and the report says so.

**Power.** D05-S5.7's reference formula fixes the *ratios* between parts;
`assets/balance/classes.json` fixes the *total* for the class. A312 makes a budget outside 5% of
that total an **error**; A210 makes a `powerCost` outside 15% of the formula a **warning**. When the
two disagree — and on a two-tonne prepared vehicle they disagree by about 1.8× — the error wins:
each part takes `target × reference_i / Σreference`, which satisfies A312 by construction.

**Drivetrain.** The chassis declares `engineForceN`, `enginePowerW` and `brakeForceN` scaled from
the vehicle's mass (8 N/kg, 90 W/kg, 10.5 N/kg), so a prepared vehicle drives the moment it loads
rather than sitting still while somebody authors an engine for it.

## Rationale / Context
The alternative for mass — believing the geometry — is DISC-035's failure, and the alternative for
power — summing the formula and letting the class target fall where it may — fails A312, which is
the gate that stops one vehicle from being quietly better than every other. Both choices are the
same shape: **derive the ratios from the model and take the totals from the content that already
balances the game.**

Every constant here is calibrated against the two shipped vehicles rather than invented, which is
also what makes them checkable: if a future car is authored by hand and disagrees with these
numbers by a lot, one of the two is wrong and the disagreement is visible.

## Impact
- `manifest.assign_masses`, `manifest.distribute_power`, `manifest.chassis_stats`.
- D15-R41 to R43 record the three rules; the report's `mass` and `balance` blocks show the working.
- A prepared pickup comes out at 2106 kg, class `heavy`, power budget exactly 84.0.
