# DEC-052: an engine is a pulse train through an exhaust, not a harmonic stack

**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S8, docs/05_vehicle_part_system.md#D05-S5.6

**Status:** active

## Summary
`SoundSynth.engineLoop` builds an engine from one unipolar exhaust pulse per firing event, placed at its crank angle and routed to its own bank, then run through three fixed exhaust formants — rather than stacking harmonics of the firing frequency. How far a V's two banks diverge is derived from `EngineConfiguration.bankFiringSpreadDegrees()`, not from a global constant. See DEC-054 for that second half.

## Details
The old model summed sine partials at `firingHz` with a 1/n falloff. That is a sawtooth, and measured it was one: the V6 loop's spectrum fell monotonically from 200 Hz with no resonant peak anywhere. Its own comment conceded this and boosted partials 2 and 3 to compensate.

Two things it cannot express, both being the character a listener identifies a car by.

**Uneven firing.** A harmonic stack at `firingHz` is perfectly even by construction. A cross-plane V8 fires evenly every 90° overall, but its banks fire `90-180-180-270` — one bank's interval sequence is the other's reversed — and the burble is the odd-order content that produces. The enum modelled this as `roughness`, a noise gain, which makes an engine hissier rather than lumpier: the ear locates unevenness in time, and a noise gain puts it in the spectrum.

**Formants.** Engine character is mostly the exhaust — a few fixed resonances the harmonics slide up through as revs rise. `EXHAUST_FORMANTS_HZ` is 105 / 560 / 1750 Hz for a reference eight-cylinder, scaled by `(8/cylinders)^0.35`, so a V12 sits lower than an I4. Exponent 0.35 rather than 1.0 because pipe resonance follows length, and length grows far more slowly than cylinder count.

The pulse is unipolar because exhaust pressure is: it pushes and never pulls. The resonators strip the DC, but the asymmetry is what gives them real low-frequency energy to ring on; a symmetric pulse produces a thinner engine.

## Rationale / Context
The concrete future failure: somebody adds a flat-plane V8 or a 60° V6 and reaches for `roughness` to make it sound different, because that is what the field used to be for. The answer is now the firing geometry — `firingAngles` and `bankOf` — and `roughness` means only combustion noise.

`bankFiringSpreadDegrees()` is measured over `bankOf` rather than authored, so it cannot drift from the pulse train the synthesiser lays down. That is what makes it safe to assert on.

## Impact
- `shared-models` `EngineConfiguration`: gains `firingAngles()`, `bankOf`, `bankCount`, `bankFiringSpreadDegrees()`, `CYCLE_DEGREES`, `cycleHzAt`. Loses `harmonics()`; `roughness()` narrows to combustion noise.
- `asset-pipeline` `SoundSynth`: `engineLoop` re-signatured on `(EngineConfiguration, rpm, seed)`; new `engineRamp`, `addExhaustPulse`, `resonate`, `formantScale`.
- All six `engine_loop_*.wav` regenerated and audibly different.
- `SoundBankTest` gains `onlyTheCrossPlaneV8FiresUnevenlyWithinABank` and `engineLoopsHaveExhaustFormants`.
