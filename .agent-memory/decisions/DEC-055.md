# DEC-055: engines get their own audio device and mixer, not libGDX Sound handles

**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S8, docs/02_technical_architecture.md#D02-S4.5

**Status:** active

## Summary
Engine voices are rendered into `EngineMixer` and written to a `Gdx.audio.newAudioDevice(48000, false)` stereo sink on a dedicated daemon thread. Every other sound in the bank stays on libGDX `Sound` exactly as it was. Positioning — distance, pan, air absorption, propagation delay and the doppler that falls out of it — is ours, applied inside the mixer.

## Details
libGDX offers three ways to get synthesised PCM out, and only the first is backend-agnostic.

**`AudioDevice` plus our own mixer (chosen).** A blocking stereo sink; the block write paces the thread, so no timer and no drift. It costs us the mixing, which is barely a cost — the spatial model we want is not OpenAL's anyway.

The two rejected: subclassing `OpenALMusic` gets an OpenAL source per voice but is lwjgl3-specific and `Music` exposes no pan; driving LWJGL's OpenAL directly means owning a second audio backend.

**Propagation delay rather than a doppler multiplier.** Each voice is read out of a delay line `distance / 343` seconds late. When a car closes the read pointer outruns the write pointer, which *is* doppler at the correct ratio, with no ratio computed anywhere. Scaling pitch instead could only be applied to the synthesiser's rpm — changing the engine's speed rather than its pitch, and leaving the fixed resonances behind. The price is 28,000 floats a voice.

**Two threads, no locks.** The simulation publishes immutable `VoiceUpdate` records into an `AtomicReferenceArray`; the audio thread reads the latest and never blocks, never allocates and never iterates a collection. A dropped update is one frame of staleness on a value that is smoothed anyway. Slots are a fixed array of 24 rather than a map, so the audio thread's scan is a bounded loop over an array.

## Rationale / Context
The failure this avoids is the ordinary one for game audio: a mixer that allocates or locks on the audio thread and drops out under GC at exactly the moment the most is happening. Measured, 24 voices cost 24% of one core, and a 256-frame block costs 1.29 ms against a 5.33 ms deadline.

Disposal order matters: the bus stops before slots are released, because releasing a slot out from under a live `render` is the one ordering that can tear a block (G19).

## Impact
- New `EngineMixer`, `EngineAudioOutput` in `game-client` `audio`.
- `AudioSystem` acquires a slot per vehicle instead of two `Sound.loop` handles, and sets the listener from the local player's transform each frame.
- D15-R37a4 added: synthesised engines are positionless and the runtime must place them.
- T-D15-14 and T-D15-15 cover the doppler and the 24-voice mix.
