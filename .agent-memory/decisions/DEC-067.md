# DEC-067: a part's mass is its surface times a per-class areal density

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S5.8, docs/09_blender_destruction_tool.md#D09-S6.2

**Status:** active

## Summary
The per-class mass constant is a **mass per square metre**, not a wall thickness times the
material's density, and the enclosed volume caps the result. DEC-064's mass formula is
superseded; the rest of that entry stands unchanged.

## Details
DEC-064 weighed a part as `area × wall thickness × density`, the thickness per destruction class
(D15-R33). On the two shipped cars that produced a **214 kg brake hub** and inflated the 1,500 kg
Eclipse to 1,977 kg.

The arithmetic is not subtle once seen. 20 mm is the right wall for a rubber tyre and gives
`0.02 × 1100 = 22 kg/m²`; the same 20 mm of *steel* gives `157 kg/m²`. One `rigid` class covers
both, so a constant expressed as a **thickness** is multiplied by whichever density the label
implies and lands an order of magnitude out on one of them.

Expressed as an areal density the constant is stable, and for a reason: a designer picks whatever
thickness gets the required stiffness out of the material to hand, which puts nearly everything on
a vehicle in a narrow band. Measured against the shipped content:

| Part | Area | Authored mass | kg/m² |
|---|---|---|---|
| door | 1.50 m² | 29 kg | 19 |
| wheel | 2.19 m² | 37.5 kg | 17 |
| hub assembly | ~1.4 m² | ~26 kg | 19 |

Glass is genuinely lighter (12.5) and a chassis heavier (78.5), being box sections and a floor
pan rather than a skin.

The cap earns its place: **a part cannot contain more material than fits inside it.** An open
surface encloses nothing and keeps the surface reading; a hollow box encloses more than its walls
hold, so the surface reading already wins; a small solid lump encloses less than its folded
surface implies, and the lump wins.

After the change, on the Eclipse: wheels 36 and 39 kg against an authored 37.5, hubs 21 and 25,
total 1,619 kg, class `medium`, budget 74.0 — the hand-authored car's class and budget.

## Rationale / Context
The failure mode is what makes this worth an entry rather than a diff. Nothing errors: the
manifest validates, the assembly loads, the mass is positive and the car drives — heavily, with
half its weight in its brake hubs, and the only symptom is that it handles wrong. A pipeline that
guesses masses must be calibrated against something known, and the two shipped vehicles are the
only such things this project has.

`WALL_THICKNESS_M` still exists and is still per class, because one place genuinely needs a
thickness: the wall each glass shard is given by the shell fracture (D09-S5.2.1). That one is a
geometric thickness rather than a mass proxy — exactly the distinction this entry is about — and
the two agree by construction, since the fracture is handed this same constant.

## Impact
- `manifest.surface_mass_kg` and `AREAL_DENSITY_KG_PER_M2` replace the thickness arithmetic.
- D15-R41 rewritten; D15-R41a adds the track-based footprint that feeds the target mass.
- Supersedes DEC-064's mass rule only. DEC-064 is marked superseded by this entry.
