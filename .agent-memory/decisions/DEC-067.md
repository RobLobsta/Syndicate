# DEC-067: a part's mass is its surface times a per-class areal density

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S5.8, docs/09_blender_destruction_tool.md#D09-S6.2

**Status:** active

## Summary
The per-class mass constant is a **mass per square metre**, not a wall thickness multiplied by
the material's density, and the enclosed volume caps the result. DEC-064's mass formula is
superseded; everything else in that entry — the chassis taking the balance, the footprint
target, the distributed power budget — stands unchanged.

## Details
DEC-064 weighed a part as `area × wall thickness × density`, with the thickness per destruction
class (D15-R33). Run on the two shipped cars, that produced a **214 kg brake hub** and inflated
the 1,500 kg Eclipse to 1,977 kg.

The arithmetic is not subtle once seen. 20 mm is the right wall for a rubber tyre and gives
`0.02 × 1100 = 22 kg/m²`. The same 20 mm of *steel* gives `0.02 × 7850 = 157 kg/m²`. One
`rigid` class covers a tyre and a steel casting, so a constant expressed as a **thickness** is
multiplied by whichever density the label happens to imply and lands an order of magnitude out
on one of the two.

Expressed as an areal density the constant is stable, and stable for a reason: a designer picks
whatever thickness gets the required stiffness out of the material to hand, which puts nearly
everything on a vehicle between 17 and 20 kg/m². Measured against the shipped content — a
1.5 m² door at 29 kg is 19, a 2.19 m² wheel at 37.5 kg is 17, a hub assembly at ~26 kg is 19.
Glass is genuinely lighter (12.5) and a chassis genuinely heavier (78.5), being box sections and
a floor pan rather than a skin.

The cap survives from the first attempt and earns its place: **a part cannot contain more
material than fits inside it.** An open surface encloses nothing and keeps the surface reading;
a hollow box encloses far more than its walls hold, so the surface reading is already the
smaller; a small solid lump encloses less than its folded surface implies, and the lump wins.

After the change, on the Eclipse: wheels 36 and 39 kg against the 37.5 kg the shipped part is
authored at, hubs 21 and 25, total 1,619 kg, class `medium` and power budget 74.0 — the same
class and the same budget as the hand-authored car.

## Rationale / Context
The failure mode is what makes this worth an entry rather than a diff. Nothing errors: the
manifest validates, the assembly loads, the mass is positive and the car drives — heavily, with
half its weight in its brake hubs, and the only symptom is that it handles wrong. A pipeline
that guesses masses has to be calibrated against something that is known, and the two shipped
vehicles are the only such things this project has.

`WALL_THICKNESS_M` still exists and is still per class, because a thickness is genuinely needed
in one place: the solidify that gives a `glass` part a wall before it is fractured. That one is
a geometric thickness rather than a mass proxy, which is exactly the distinction this entry is
about.

## Impact
- `manifest.surface_mass_kg` and `AREAL_DENSITY_KG_PER_M2` replace the thickness arithmetic.
- D15-R41 rewritten; D15-R41a adds the track-based footprint that feeds the target mass.
- Supersedes DEC-064's mass rule only. DEC-064 is marked superseded by this entry.
