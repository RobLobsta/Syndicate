# DEC-080: a weapon is an assembly, and its sub-parts hang off a synthesised mount

**Date:** 2026-08-16
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/17_weapon_system.md#D17-S4.2, docs/17_weapon_system.md#D17-S5.8, docs/05_vehicle_part_system.md#D05-S4.3

**Status:** active

## Summary
A modular weapon is a **slot graph**, not a part: a `mount` occupying the vehicle's hardpoint, with
a `receiver`, `barrel`, `muzzle`, `breech`, `feed`, `gear`, `sight` and `furniture` hanging off it on
`SUBSLOT`s, each with its own mass, health and damage state. The `mount` carries the `weapon` block
and is the part that fires; every other sub-part is a target that can be shot off independently.
Where a model supplies no mount — which is most gun models — one is **synthesised as real geometry**.

## Details
The taxonomy is closed (D17-R2), in the mould of D15-R1, and each label carries a category, a slot
type, a destruction class and a default material. Only the labels that make the gun *work* are
`WEAPON`: a muzzle brake, a sight and a grip are `DECORATIVE`, and losing them is cosmetic.

**The mount holds the `weapon` block and nothing else does.** It is the part that occupies the
vehicle's slot, so it is the thing that was "fitted"; A216 makes a weapon block on anything else an
error, and D08-R5's "a part in category weapon with no block never fires" is then exactly right for
the sub-parts — they do not fire, the gun does.

**Synthesising the mount is the common path, not the exception.** A gun is usually modelled without
whatever it bolts to. The boss is generated as real geometry — it renders, collides and fractures
like any other part — sized to the receiver it carries, spanning from the model's floor up into that
receiver so the join is a real contact rather than two faces that happen to meet.

Sub-parts join the vehicle's existing compound shape as further children (D17-R45), so a fitted
weapon costs one child hull per sub-part and no extra bodies. DEC-004 is unchanged.

## Rationale / Context
The alternative — one weapon part with articulated sub-meshes — was on the table and was rejected by
the user. It is much cheaper and it collapses the whole feature: a gun that can only be destroyed as
a unit is a health pool with a muzzle flash, and "guns get damaged and destroyed logically" cannot be
built on it. The subtree rule then gives the rest for free: destroying the mount takes the whole
weapon, because D07-S5.7 already removes a part's descendants.

## Impact
- Two weapons ship as five and six parts respectively, in `assets/parts/` (the shared library).
- `GaragePreview` had to learn to walk the slot chain; it previously drew only chassis children and
  silently omitted 4 of 36 parts.
- A vehicle's `expected.totalMassKg`, `comLocal` and `powerBudget` all move when a weapon is fitted,
  and three calibration tests had to learn the difference between "the car" and "what it carries".
