# DEC-048: gamepad and keyboard are peers, and the active one is observed

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/04_entity_component_model.md#D04-S4.4, docs/03_runtime_modes.md#D03-S4.1

**Status:** active

## Summary
`InputCollectionSystem` (slot 1) polls both a `GamepadSource` and a `KeyboardMouseSource` every frame and keeps the output of whichever device the player last touched. Neither is primary. Tuning is content, in `assets/input/bindings.json`.

## Details

No blueprint specifies input devices — D04-S4.4 row 1 says only "reads device state, writes `PlayerInputComponent`" — so this is recorded under CLAUDE.md rule 6, not as a deviation.

**Which device is live is observed, not configured.** A settings screen asks a question the game can see the answer to, and handles worst the case that happens: somebody who drives with a pad and types with a keyboard, switching several times a minute. The router switches when the rival exceeds a threshold *and* the active device has been quiet for half a second — hysteresis, because a stick is never perfectly still.

**Both are polled every frame and one is kept.** Polling only the active source cannot work: nothing would see the other device move, so nothing could switch to it.

**Neither translates through the other**, which is the substance of "peers". A stick reports a continuous position and needs a dead zone and a curve; a key reports on or off and needs a *ramp* to become steering at all. Held steering approaches full lock over about 0.4 s and recentres in 0.17 s — asymmetric on purpose. That ramp is what makes a keyboard driveable at speed rather than a series of stabs the car never settles between.

Two details easy to get wrong:

- **The dead zone rescales what is left.** Zeroing alone steps the axis from 0 to 0.18 as the stick crosses, and the car twitches.
- **Keyboard activity is measured from the keys, not the ramped axis.** A player who just pressed a key has full intent and a near-zero axis; reading the axis would leave the keyboard dead for a second after every switch.

**gdx-controllers 2.x has no portable trigger axes** — `ControllerMapping` maps both triggers to the digital `buttonL2`/`buttonR2`, and analogue indices vary by pad and platform. Those indices are content, defaulting to the digital button. It matters: an on/off throttle on a pad is what the analogue mapping exists to avoid.

Everything touching hardware is in `LibGdxDevices` and decides nothing; every dead zone, curve and ramp is tested against a fake device.

## Rationale / Context
Input feel is the thing in a driving game most worth iterating on and least testable by inspection. A design where trying a different steering ramp needs a human with a controller is one where nobody tries one — this entry exists so the split survives the first person who finds it indirect.

## Impact
`game-client`'s new `input` package, `ClientSystemProvider`, `assets/input/bindings.json`, and `gdx-controllers` in the version catalog.
