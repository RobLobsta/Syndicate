# DEC-090: a rotor is a part category, and lift is a body force

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/05_vehicle_part_system.md#D05-S4.2, docs/06_physics_simulation.md#D06-S5.5, docs/04_entity_component_model.md#D04-S4.3.2

**Status:** active

## Summary
`PartCategory.ROTOR` and `SlotType.ROTOR_MOUNT` join the taxonomy, a `rotor` block joins `part.json`
beside `handling` and `weapon`, and a new shared operation `RotorControl` is the rotorcraft
counterpart of `VehicleControl`'s wheel path. `VehicleControl.drive` branches on a
`VehicleChassisComponent.isRotorcraft` flag set at spawn, so slot 7 and `ReconciliationSystem` (20)
cannot disagree about which arithmetic a vehicle gets (DEC-061).

## Details
The alternative considered and rejected was to make a rotor a `WHEEL` with unusual numbers, which
would have needed no new category. It fails on three counts, and each is load-bearing rather than
tidy:

1. **A wheel carries no hull** (D06-S4.3) because it is a ray cast. A rotor is a 9.45 m disc above
   the aircraft that a shot must reach, so it must be *in* the compound shape.
2. **A wheel's force acts through a contact patch**; a rotor's acts against nothing. No suspension
   ray, no grip, no surface lookup, no load transfer — and no parameterising of the wheel loop
   produces one.
3. **A wheel's direction is the vehicle's; a rotor's is its own.** Thrust along the *vehicle's* up
   axis rather than the world's is the flight model in one line — it is why pitching the nose down
   converts lift into forward flight, without that being written anywhere as a rule.

**The split between the block and the stats** follows DEC-039 exactly. `RotorBlock` carries what the
rotor *is* — role, radius, spin axis, blade count, governed rpm — because those are identities and
one-rotor properties, and summing two rotors' radii is meaningless. Thrust is a **stat**,
`ROTOR_THRUST_N`, because it is the number that must fall when the disc is shot, and degradation
(D05-S5.4) only reaches stats.

DEC-095 covers the control scheme built on top of this: the fourth input axis and the hover trim.

**Losing the tail rotor spins the aircraft, and nothing says so.** The main disc's reaction torque is
applied every tick; the tail rotor's contribution cancels it. Destroy the tail rotor and the
cancelling term is simply absent. There is no branch anywhere that tests for it — which is the
property that makes the part-based model worth having, and is why `RotorFlightTest` exists to record
it.

## Rationale / Context
The concrete future failure this stops: the next person adding an aircraft finds `WHEEL` "already
nearly works", makes a rotor a wheel with a huge friction slip, and the vehicle flies with no
collision geometry above the cabin and no way to shoot the rotor off — at which point the entire
point of the modular damage model is gone for every aircraft the game will ever have.

## Impact
`shared-models` (`PartCategory`, `DestructionClass`); `game-core` (`SlotType`, `RotorBlock`,
`RotorControl`, `RotorControllerComponent`, `StatBlock.Stat.ROTOR_THRUST_N`, `PartType`,
`AssetLoader` A223/A224, `VehicleFactory`, `VehicleControl`, `VehicleChassisComponent`,
`PlayerInputComponent`, `AssemblyValidator` A309); `asset-pipeline` (A309); `game-client`
(`PartArticulation.Driver.ROTOR`, `ArticulationState`, the keyboard and scripted input sources).
`RotorFlightTest` and `KestrelFlightTest` are the record.
