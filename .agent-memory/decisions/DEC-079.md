# DEC-079: a palette and a tone band, not just per-surface rows

**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S4.5, docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S5.9

**Status:** active

## Summary
The house style gains two mechanisms that apply to **every** surface, on top of the per-surface
rows: hues are snapped onto a six-hue palette, and finished luminance is clamped into one global
band. The style is retargeted at Crossout's wasteland — Syndicate industrial plate and Ravager
black-and-cyan — as `style_syndicate_wasteland`.

## Details
A table of per-surface rows makes each surface plausible and does nothing to make a *roster*
coherent. Two rules do:

**The palette (R47i).** Six hues — rust, sand, sodium, faction red, gunmetal, Ravager cyan — and
every hue is pulled 75% onto its nearest. A limited hue wheel is most of what an art style *is*.
The pull is partial so two reds that started apart do not finish identical, and it takes the short
way round the wheel. Lamps are exempt: a tail light is red and a machine glow is cyan whatever the
vehicles are allowed.

**The tone band (R47j).** Luminance clamped to [0.03, 0.62], and **not scaled by the
stylised/realistic strength** — a rule that applies at 62% to one model and 100% to the next is the
drift it exists to prevent. The floor keeps a black part a shape in shadow rather than a hole; the
ceiling keeps blown-out photoscan albedo from punching through a scene whose brightest natural
surface is weathered steel. Lamps are exempt from the ceiling and only the ceiling.

**Measured on what renders, not on what is written (R47k).** For a textured material the socket is
a multiplier, so the band is measured on `texture mean x factor` and the *factor* is scaled to
bring the product into range. The texture ships as the artist made it.

**Some materials cannot be fixed (R47l).** A texture darker than the floor cannot be lifted by any
multiplier. Those are reported as `unliftable` rather than treated as failures: the honest answer is
that the texture is nearly black and the remedy is a decision about the art. Two on the Eclipse,
none on the Stampede.

## Rationale / Context
The ask was specifically about brights and darks clashing and about imports that make the scene not
look right. Per-surface rows cannot express either, because both are statements about a surface's
relationship to *everything else in the frame*.

## Impact
- Both cars re-exported; the Stampede reports 0 of 24 materials outside the band, the Eclipse 2 of
  60, both unliftable.
- Every run reports its own luminance range and offender list, so drift is visible per vehicle.
- The generated terrain is **not** style-normalised and sits noticeably lighter than the vehicles.
  That palette is D16's (`ArenaTheme`), and unifying the two is a real change to a different system.
