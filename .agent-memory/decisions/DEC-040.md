# DEC-040: the damage ledger is a bucketed window on the match singleton

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/01_product_game_design.md#D01-S5.4, docs/04_entity_component_model.md#D04-S4.3.5

**Status:** active

## Summary
`DamageLedger` keeps a match-long total and a ten-second window per attacker-victim pair, the window
as ten fixed buckets of sixty ticks rather than a log of every hit. It lives in a
`DamageLedgerComponent` on the match singleton (entity 1), and D04-S4.3.5 was amended to name it.

## Details

**Two questions with different lifetimes.** D01-S5.4 needs the match total for the results screen and
the recent total for assists: a player who took a vehicle to a quarter of its hit points and
disengaged deserves credit when a teammate finishes it ten seconds later, and does not deserve it for
a hit three minutes old.

**Why buckets rather than a log.** Propagation produces several applications per hit, so a busy
ten-minute match is six figures of entries — exact, unbounded, and answering a question that only
needs a resolution of about a second. Ten buckets per pair is fixed memory, and expiry costs nothing:
a bucket stamped with a tick range outside the window reads as zero and is overwritten on its next
write. The cost is a window boundary quantised to a sixth of the window, which nobody can observe.

**Why the match singleton.** The ledger's entire purpose is to outlive the vehicle it records damage
against — the moment it is read is the moment that vehicle dies and its entity is queued for
destruction. A ledger on the victim would be gone exactly when it was needed.

**The key is packed with the attacker masked, not sign-extended.** An `EntityId` packs its generation
into the high byte (D04-R23), so an id with a generation past 127 is a negative `int`; sign-extending
it into the composite key spills into the victim's word and files the contribution under a different
vehicle. That is a bug that would appear only after 128 respawns of one entity index.

## Rationale / Context
The obvious implementation is a list of `(attacker, victim, tick, amount)` and it is the one a future
session will reach for. This records that it was considered, that its memory profile is the reason it
was not used, and that the bucketing is exact enough for the only question it answers.

## Impact
`game-core` `damage` (`DamageLedger`), `component` (`DamageLedgerComponent`), `ecs` (`WorldFactory`),
`system` (`DamageSystem`, `ScoreSystem`), and `docs/04_entity_component_model.md#D04-S4.3.5`.
