# DEC-099: a structure part's mass is its surface times a per-material areal density

**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/16_procedural_arena_generation.md#D16-S7.1, docs/09_blender_destruction_tool.md#D09-S6.2, docs/08_asset_pipeline.md#D08-S4.3

**Status:** active

## Summary
`syndicate_structure.mass` weighs a part as `area x areal density`, capped by `enclosed volume x
material density` — DEC-067's rule for vehicle parts — but off its **own** table, keyed on
**material** rather than on destruction class. Concrete is 400 kg/m², steel 60, wood 30.

## Details
The rule transfers; the numbers do not. `syndicate_prepare.manifest` carries 78.5 kg/m² for
`STRUCTURAL`, calibrated on a car's box sections and floor pan. A seven-storey block weighed at that
figure comes out at the mass of a small van, and the first symptom would be a building that a car
can shove.

Keying on material rather than on class is the other difference, and it is because a building is
**not weight-optimised**. A vehicle designer picks whatever thickness gets the required stiffness out
of the material to hand, which lands nearly everything on a car between 17 and 20 kg/m² whatever it
is made of — that stability is what makes DEC-067's class table work. A wall is as thick as it needs
to be to stand up and keep heat in, which lands concrete at 150–200 mm nearly everywhere and makes
*material* the stable axis instead.

Steel is 60 rather than a plate thickness, and that is not a fudge: a detailed mesh counts both faces
of every tube, so a nominal 7.6 mm reads as about 15 mm of real wall. Measured on the shipped
content: the turret is 411 t over five parts, the six-storey block 336 t over three.

`HP_PER_KG` is 0.06, an order of magnitude below the vehicle table's 2.0, for the arithmetic reason:
a 90-tonne floor at 2 hp/kg needs 180,000 points of damage — about nine minutes of sustained cannon
fire. At 0.06 it is 5,400 hp, which is a couple of rocket volleys.

## Rationale / Context
Two independent mass tables is exactly the kind of duplication that drifts, so the reason for a
second one has to be stated once and be a real reason. It is: the quantity that is stable across a
car is not the quantity that is stable across a building, and using one table for both means one of
the two is wrong by an order of magnitude.

## Impact
- `materials.json` gains `concrete` (2400 kg/m³) and `wood` (700), with `COMPOSITE` audio because
  the bank has no masonry or timber set and adding one is a content change, not a material one.
- A structure part's break impulse is 45 N·s/kg against a vehicle part's 16: a structure is bolted
  down, and a car hitting a building at 20 m/s delivers about 30,000 N·s.
