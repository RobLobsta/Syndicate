# DEC-046: the sound bank is synthesised, and the reason is the licence

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Category:** decisions
**Related Docs:** docs/15_vehicle_preparation_pipeline.md#D15-S8, docs/08_asset_pipeline.md#D08-S4.6

**Status:** active

## Summary
Every sound in `assets/audio/` is generated by `asset-pipeline`'s `SoundBankBuilder` rather than sampled or downloaded. D15-R38 ranks procedural synthesis second behind permissively licensed libraries; the bank takes it first, for every family rather than only the loops.

## Details

**The deciding argument is D15-R39's, not D15-R38's.** Every audio asset has to record a licence, and the two shipped vehicle models are already CC-BY-NC-SA — a constraint that is live and that nobody wants a second, differently-encumbered version of. A synthesised bank carries the repository's own terms, needs no attribution, and cannot acquire an incompatible clause when somebody swaps a file.

Three properties follow and all three are checked by `SoundBankTest`:

- **Reproducible.** The same seed produces byte-identical files, so the committed bank is exactly what the code says it is and a regeneration is a no-op diff rather than a review of forty binaries.
- **Seamless loops.** D15-R38 rules generative audio out for loops because of the seam. A synthesised loop has none by construction: the buffer holds a whole number of firing cycles, so every harmonic completes a whole number of cycles in it, and the noise component's join is crossfaded with equal power. Measured, the step at the join is smaller than the buffer's own 95th-percentile adjacent-sample step in every loop.
- **Parametric.** Which is what D15-S8 asks of the engine and tyre loops anyway.

**Impacts are modal, not filtered noise.** A struck object is a broadband transient over a handful of exponentially damped, inharmonic sinusoids. Filtering noise is easier to write and does not work: the ear identifies material from decay time and partial spacing far more than from spectral tilt. Measured on the shipped bank — metal decays over 1.2–4.3 s with a zero-crossing rate of 217–742 Hz; rubber decays in 0.08–0.29 s at 109–354 Hz; glass is 1867–6450 Hz. Those are the differences a listener names as "metal", "thud" and "glass".

**Severity is authored, never pitched.** Three impact variants per material differ in modal frequency, decay and brightness, because a harder hit excites a larger area and therefore rings *lower*. Pitch-shifting one recording gives a chipmunk, which is the most recognisable sign of a cheap bank.

49 files, 4.3 MB, covering nine event families over five audio materials, four destruction classes, three vehicle classes, three surfaces and seven weapon families.

## Rationale / Context
The obvious future move is "replace these with real recordings", and it is not obviously wrong — but it costs the licence property, which is the thing that was actually bought here. A session that makes that trade should make it knowingly, and this entry is what makes that possible.

## Impact
`asset-pipeline`'s new `audio` package, `assets/audio/`, `AudioEvent` and `AudioMaterial` in `shared-models`, and the `buildSoundBank` Gradle task. D15-R38 gains R38a, R38b and R38c in the same commit.
