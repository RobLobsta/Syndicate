# DEV-007: TestWorld builds its own Bullet world at 0.005 m, not via PhysicsWorld.create

**Date:** 2026-08-08
**Category:** spec_deviations
**Related Docs:** docs/14_test_environment.md#D14-S5.5, docs/06_physics_simulation.md#D06-S5.1

**Status:** resolved

## Summary
D14-R10 requires the harness to construct its Bullet world through `game-core`'s `PhysicsWorld.create()`, and D06-R13 fixes the convex collision margin at 0.01 m. `TestWorld` did neither: it built its own world at 0.005 m, on Bullet's default solver settings. **Resolved 2026-08-08** — it delegates to `PhysicsWorld`, and its margin constant is an alias of the game's.

## Details

**Deviated from:** the harness-uses-the-game's-world rule (D14-R10), checked as AC-D14-10, and the 0.01 m margin (D06-R13). `TestWorld` predated `PhysicsWorld`, so there was nothing to construct through; once `PhysicsWorld` existed the gap became a real divergence. The margin is the half that matters — it is the distance a resting shard sits above the ground, and the physics checks measure exactly that.

**Resolution.** `TestWorld` holds a `PhysicsWorld` and delegates the world, gravity, solver, step and body membership to it, adding only a ground box, hull construction from `MeshData`, and body ownership. Bodies go on the layer their mass implies (`STATIC` at 0, else `DEBRIS`, D06-S4.4).

**The blocker was one unused dependency.** `test-environment` would not compile here because `libs.gdx.gltf` resolves only from JitPack, which this environment blocks — yet no source imports gdx-gltf. DEC-008 made it dead; the declaration outlived it. Removed, with D02-S4.5 amended in the same commit (DEC-013).

**Measured shift:** a 1 m cube dropped from 2 m rested at **0.505000 m** at the old margin and rests at **0.510000 m** at the game's — exactly the +0.005 m predicted.

**No fixture expectation needed updating**, which is the part worth remembering. PHYS-008 computes its expected height as `-min.y + TestWorld.COLLISION_MARGIN_M`, so it always measured the harness against the harness's own margin and always agreed; `fixtures/golden/` is empty (D14-S7.2 not started), so nothing else pinned a height. A wrong constant that every check derives from is invisible to all of them.

**New coverage:** `TestWorldTest` (L2) compares the two margin constants directly rather than deriving from either, reads the solver settings back off the live world (AC-D14-10), and drops a synthetic cube to assert it rests at half-extent + one margin. No Blender required.

## Rationale / Context
A future session adding a solver setting to `PhysicsWorld` would have assumed the harness inherited it, because D14-R10 says it does — and the margin gap showed up only as a half-centimetre offset between a capture and the game, reading as a rendering problem.

## Impact
`test-environment`: `TestWorld`, `CheckRunner`'s PHYS-008/009, `build.gradle.kts`.

**Blueprint Disposition: IMPL_SHOULD_CHANGE — done.** The documents were right; the implementation moved. `:test-environment:check` green, 16 tests.

## Follow-ups
`:test-environment:verifyFixtures` has not been re-run at the new margin — it needs Blender, absent here. Every fixture's resting height moves up 5 mm; PHYS-008 should still pass, since its expectation moves with the constant. Re-run before trusting a fixture report predating this change.
