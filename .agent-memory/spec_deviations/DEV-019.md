# DEV-019: airborne, the speed clamp bounds the horizontal component, not the whole vector

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Category:** spec_deviations
**Related Docs:** docs/06_physics_simulation.md#D06-S5.5, docs/00_master_index.md#D00-S5.2

**Status:** active

## Summary
D06-S5.5's clamp rescales the entire velocity vector to `MAX_VEHICLE_SPEED_MPS`. Applied to an
airborne vehicle already at the horizontal limit, it removes whatever gravity adds, so the car
cannot accelerate downward. It is now applied to the horizontal component only while no wheel is in
contact. Grounded behaviour is unchanged.

## Details

**Deviates from:** the anti-tunnelling clamp in D06-S5.5's pseudocode, which sits under the
ray-cast vehicle requirement (D06-R18):

    if speed > MAX_VEHICLE_SPEED_MPS (40):
        vehicle.body.setLinearVelocity(normalize(v) * MAX_VEHICLE_SPEED_MPS)

**What changed:** `VehicleControl.applySpeedClamp` branches on whether any live wheel reports
contact. Grounded, the line above runs exactly as written. Airborne, only the `xz` component is
scaled and `y` is left to gravity.

**Why the spec's version misbehaves.** Normalising preserves *direction*, so a pinned vehicle does
not stop descending — the vector rotates toward straight down at constant magnitude. Two wrong
consequences, neither obvious from the pseudocode:

1. A car cannot fall faster than 40 m/s *total*, so with 40 m/s of horizontal speed it has none left
   for falling and hangs. DISC-063 measured 130 frames of flight reading exactly 40 m/s.
2. Horizontal speed *bleeds away* in flight, because rotating a fixed-magnitude vector downward
   takes it out of `xz`. A jump costs forward progress no force applied.

**What is unchanged:** the clamp's purpose. D06-S5.5 calls it "a second net" behind CCD against a
chassis tunnelling through geometry, and tunnelling is translation through the world — the
horizontal term for a car on an arena floor. That bound is still enforced either way.

A vehicle with no live wheel falls back to the whole-vector clamp: that is a wreck on its belly
(D05-E1), not a car in flight.

## Rationale / Context
Recorded rather than fixed quietly because the pseudocode is unambiguous, and a future session
reading `applySpeedClamp` against it would see a contradiction and "correct" it back — restoring a
bug that eleven suites missed and only driving found.

For whoever amends the document: the obvious test does **not** discriminate. Asserting that a
launched car descends passes under both behaviours. The signature is total speed pinned at the limit.

## Impact
`game-core` `vehicle` (`VehicleControl`), `AirborneSpeedClampTest`.

**Blueprint Disposition: DOC_SHOULD_CHANGE.** D06-S5.5 should say the clamp bounds horizontal speed,
and why: the invariant it defends is against translation through geometry, and gravity must never be
a term the simulation may discard. As written it makes the desert's own dune field unplayable.
